<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up around 7am to get ready and went upstairs at around 7:30 to eat breakfast. We ate the typical eggs and toast again (breakfast was free with the hotel). After breakfast we walked down to the tour agency shop to get fitted for ice climbing gear. The tour shop was not open when we arrived (at 8am), our friend Maria was joining us for the climbing and was at the café next door to the tour agency so we met her over there. After ~30 minutes we were able to connect with the tour company and get our gear all sorted out before loading up into the car for another car ride. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ride out to the mountain, Huyana potosi took forever. We left the city around 9am and didn’t get to the base camp until almost ~12. It didn’t help that we had to wait at least 30 minutes for our guide to 'pick up food for us' (He claimed it was difficult to find gluten free food). It also didn’t help that our van got stuck on one of the muddy roads on our way out to the mountain 😂. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/nv04uyS.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption>Notice Becca supervising from the van window :)</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our driver seemingly had no idea what to do when your car gets stuck in the mud. He decided that the best course of action would be to try and keep revving the car and spinning the wheels as much as possible to try to get us out (of course this just made the van get more stuck). Finally he listened to me and we put a bunch of rocks and whatever else we could find under the wheel while us (and some passerby's who stopped to help us) pushed the car out of the mud.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Near the base of the mountain we stopped at a famous cemetery for miners and there families. Apparently there was a pretty big mine out here that had an accident and killed a lot of the workers. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>We ended up leaving around 3:30 to try and make it back to La Paz in time for the cholitas wrestling show. The cholitas are the Andean women who dress in traditional clothes, they are usually in the street shops selling their goods (you can't mistake them). The wrestling match was basically a knock-off WWE show. It was hilarious and a bit hard to follow, but basically 5 cholitas were in the ring at one time all “fighting” each other. At a few points they even got out of the ring and were fighting each other in the stands!</p>

<p>Our driver drove us back to our hotel after the show. We dropped off our bags and then walked down to the cafe we were at earlier for dinner. It was a really neat restaurant with foods from all over the world. I had the Bali bowl and it was really good! After dinner we made our way back to the hotel to take our Covid tests. Of course there were issues with emed, but I was able to work around them and both of our tests came back negative :). We packed up our stuff and went to bed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Day 10 Flight Home</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We woke up just after 2am to catch a taxi (pre-arranged) to take us to the airport. We had no issues catching our 5 am flight to Lima. After a short layover in Lima our flight to Atlanta left on time. We knew there was an earlier flight from Atlanta to Savannah so when we landed we hurried to the Delta desk and they were able to get us on the earlier flight...or so we thought. It turns out we were just on standby for the early flight and after everyone boarded only Becca was cleared (there was only 1 seat left). So we ended up both staying  and getting dinner together at buffalo wild wings before our later flight (about 3 hours later). We finally made it back to Savannah and back home around 11:30 pm, talk about a long travel day!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As always I'm forever grateful of the amazing travel opportunities and experiences I'm able to have with such awesome people. Definitely wish we had more time to explore Bolivia, but I think we were able to fit about as much as possible (and some!). For now, I need another vacation to rest! Until next time.</p>
