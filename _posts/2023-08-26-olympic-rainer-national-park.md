<p>Here's a quick re-cap of our annual national park hiking weekend trip. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Day 0 - Thursday</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Becca had the day off and I worked from home until ~1:30 when we loaded up the car and drove up to Charleston airport (we got a cheap direct flight to Seattle in Alaskan Air). The drive up took about 2 hours and by the time we got through security they were starting to board. The flight took about 5.5 hours and we arrived at 7:30 pm. There were some great views of Mount Adam’s and Mount Rainer on our approach into SeaTac. </p>

<p>Once we arrived we grabbed our car rental (a Ford Bronco) and drove another ~2 hours to our Airbnb in Sequim (pronounced “swim”). We arrived around 10 pm and went to bed. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We were originally hoping to stay with and visit Becca’s sister, however they ended up moving to North Carolina like a month before our trip! :(</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Day 1</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up pretty early (3 hour time zone difference helps). I made a quick trip to Walmart to pick up some snacks and breakfast food while Becca napped. We cooked eggs, sausages, and bagels for breakfast. We got ready and had a bit of time to spare so we played a Sequim version of monopoly our Airbnb had before heading out around 8:40. We didn't get to finish the game but it's basically the same as monopoly just themed for Sequim.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://i.imgur.com/abQK2ob.jpg","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"embed-handler","responsive":true} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We drove about 10 min to the Sequim valley airport where I was going to do the wing walking! Wing walking is pretty much exactly what it sounds - you get to walk on the wing of an airplane while it's flying and then do a bunch of aerobatic maneuvers. I think saw it online somewhere and when I found out they did it in Seattle I couldn't pass up the opportunity. Luckily I have an awesome supportive wife who was happy to let me do it (as long as my life insurance was up to date). Once we arrived we met Marlyin (the owner) and her daughter at the airplanes to sign all the paper work and then start training. It's a small family run operation and they were super personable and friendly.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The training consisted of lots of practice knowing where to step, how to climb up, and how to use the buckles (for the top position). Becca ended up going to do another short trail while I did the training. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://i.imgur.com/hej39PE.jpg","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"embed-handler","responsive":true} -->
<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-rich is-provider-embed-handler wp-block-embed-embed-handler"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper">

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ran through the movements about a dozen times each. There were 5 other people there, one girl ended up leaving early because she could barely get on top of the wing while it was on the ground (they don't charge you anything until right before it's time to fly because they don't want people to feel financial pressure to do it). Two of the other people were from Chicago, one from India, and one from Brazil. Luckily I was going to be the first to go (because I was the first to contact them about booking). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Unfortunately, there was a bit of rain so we were delayed a few hours. Thankfully the weather cleared up after lunch. Becca ended up getting back from her hike and bringing me a hot dog from Costco to eat. We finally ended up starting the plane around 3pm, after warming up the engines I got in the front seat and we taxied for takeoff! </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The plane was really cool, an old open cockpit Stearman (a bi-plane) I decided to do both the upper and lower wing positions. Getting up on top and strapping in was the scariest part (you're literally "held on" by a small cable and a carabiner/harness). Also reassuringly when I asked about the procedure if you were to fall Marilyn said "don't fall" and there was no procedure besides trying. to pull yourself back up. Once I was strapped I gave Mike (the pilot) the signal and we did 6 aerobatic maneuvers (loop, hammerhead, and barrel roll). The loop was the most intense because you pull 2-3 Gs (as if my legs weren't shaking enough just being up there). The hammerhead was also fun because you get weightless (0 G) at the top of the maneuver.</p>
