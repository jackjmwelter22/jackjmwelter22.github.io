<p>We met our guide, Lola, at the main square of Santa Teresa along with the rest of the tour group, about 30 people (fairly big). We started the tour walking down the streets of the town. Our guide pointed out some artwork on the walls, the town is known for it's street art. I learned that pucca shells represent the aboriginals in brazil - apparently aboriginal fortune tellers use them to tell fortunes. There is a lot of prejudice against the aboriginals in Brazil and the artwork we saw was political about equality for the aboriginals..</p>

<p>After continuing through the streets we stopped at a small park. Our tour guide pointed out a special tree - the Pau Brazil - which is where Brazil got its' name from. The wood produces purple and red dye, which was used for royalty very rare and expensive at that time and the tree was abundant along the Brazilian coast. In the 1500s the Portuguese explorers would cut down the trees for exportation. The word "brasa" means ember or red hot coal - referring to the color of the dye extracted from the tree and the word eventually morphed into Brazil. Also according to our guide the best violins bows are made from the wood.</p>

<p>After lunch we walked about 10 minutes down the hill to see the Selaron steps. We accidentally arrived at the top of the steps, so we had to walk down them for the full effect. The project to revive the dilapidated steps began in 1990 by a local artist. He started by using tiles discarded from construction sites but later began incorporating tiles sent by visitors from around the world. The stairs have more than 2,000 brightly colored tiles from over 60 countries. It was really cool to see, but the steps were very crowded!</p>
