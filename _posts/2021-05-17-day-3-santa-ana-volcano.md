<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up early again and had breakfast at the hotel at 7am. Breakfast was included which was nice and consisted of scrambled eggs, bread,  and fried plantains. After breakfast we loaded up to drive (about an hour) to the Santa Ana volcano for a hike. Unfortunately even though the volcano is literally right next to where we’re staying we had to drive all the way around the lake to get there because it’s the only road. We got to a place called castle cristal at 8:30 when we were supposed to meet our guide (who we talked to on the phone the day before, he was recommended by our hotel). Not sure what happened but our guide wasn’t there and didn’t answer any of our calls or messages. Luckily we were able to find another guide but had to wait an hour before starting the hike. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Unfortunately it’s not allowed to hike by yourself here. Instead we had to hike with a group of about 9 other people, 2 guides, and an armed police officer. Of course no one the other people in our group were in that great of shape so it was going to be a very slowww hike up. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>No more than 10 minutes into the hike we took our first break, most of the rest of the group was already out of breath. Luckily our guide allowed us to continue on with the police officer so that we didn’t have to wait for everyone. The police officer was super chatty! All in Spanish of course so it was good practice for me. He talked about everything from history of the volcano, to family/job, the pandemic, the economy, and even politics (he’s surprisingly pro trump and thinks trump will get re-elected next election haha). After about an hour and a half of hiking we made it to the top! There were some really good views of the lagoon in the crater! The policeman joked that if you can smell the sulfur then you’re COVID negative. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The temperature of the lagoon is more than 125F so no swimming allowed! The last time the volcano erupted was in 2005. We hung out at the top for about 20 minutes or so before the rest of the group caught back up to us. We took some more pictures, you could even see the lake we’re staying at with the little island that we kayaked around. I found out from the policeman that the island is mostly owned by one of El Salvador’s ex-presidents. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After spending some time at the top the guide let Becca and I head down ahead of everyone else since we were faster. It probably took about 30 min to get back to the car. We drove back towards the hotel and stopped at a restaurant on the lake, the food there was much better than our hotel. We both ended up getting the fajitas. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1922,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we got back to our hotel, hung out at the pool for a little and then took the kayaks out again. This time we went searching for the “hot spring” which was close to where we were staying. Unfortunately I didn’t have my phone but the “hot spring” was comically sad. It was basically a couple holes (no more than 2 feet in diameter) on the beach with puddles in them with no more than an inch of water. To be fair the water was pretty hot, but it was not at all what we were expecting haha! We’re planning on going to another hot spring tomorrow, hopefully it’s nicer!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After kayaking we hung out on our balcony before eating some papusas and then going to bed. </p>
