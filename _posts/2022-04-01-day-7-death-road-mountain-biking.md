<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The overnight bus from Uyuni arrived super early at the La Paz station (4:45am instead of 6:30am). We were originally supposed to be picked up for our bike tour directly from the bus station, but because we had so much time we decided to take a cab 5 min to our hotel instead (staying at the Quantu hotel again) to drop our bags off and got some sleep. We were able to get a quick nap in, woke up around 7, and were picked up by our tour guide in the lobby at 7:10. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our tour guide, Nelson aka Nel, was super energetic and fun. If you ever go to ride the death road I highly recommend him (he works for Altitude). Our group was the 4 of us, a Brit named Thomas, a German guy (who already rode the death road 20 years prior), and A girl who just graduated named kiara, they were all super nice and good company and lots of random funny and deep conversations. We made our way by bus about an hour and a half up into the mountains north of La Paz to the start of the tour (almost 5000m elevation). We got suited up with all our gear, took some pictures, and got the safety explanation from Nel. </p>

<p>The first part of the road was the new road which was paved and had a decent amount of traffic. We were able to go pretty fast in this part because the road, for the most part, was smooth and well maintained. I believe this section was about 30km total which we broke up into multiple stops so the group didn’t spread out too much. At this altitude riding was actually kinda cold, and between stops I had to try to warm up my hands because they were freezing! One thing I really appreciated was they didn’t make us all ride together in one group, everyone was able to ride at the pace they wanted/were comfortable with and meet up at each stop. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the paved section of the road we ended at a tunnel which had a small paved detour rode around it to get a taste of how the bikes handle on a much much bumpier road (what we were about to be riding on exclusively). Then we loaded up the bikes and drove to the real death road about 10 minutes away. On the way there our guide played the song highway to hell 😅. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once at the start of the roads we unloaded the bikes again and got another safety explanation and a small history lesson. Bolivia has been in 3 wars, all of which they lost (Brazil, Chile, and Paraguay I think). During their war with Paraguay they had a lot of prisoners of war which they put to work to build the road which, according to our guide, is why the road is so shitty haha. This part of the road was much tighter and way more bumpy than the new road (as expected).  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This section of the road was also much longer, I have no clue how long it was, but we broke it up into much more sections than the paved road. There were a few scary parts on the ride down, Sarah ended up riding in the van for a bit (which was following us) because she wasn’t comfortable biking (honestly though the van was probably scarier than the bikes!). There were a lot of cool stops with good views. Unfortunately at the higher altitudes it was much more foggy and difficult to see but as we got lower and lower the clouds disappeared and the temperature got way warmer. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ended up biking through waterfalls and rivers which was fun. I ended up riding up front with the guide and we were whipping down the mountain fast, it was a blast! At one point we had to stop to remove some boulders from the road so our van (which was trailing us) could make it past. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stopped at the San Juan falls and saw the memorial for the last accident on the death road, it was a tour bus filled with Israeli tourists that fell off the cliff (which is why the memorial is in Hebrew. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our next stop was for a snack at a small viewpoint/hut. Thomas had a drone and was able to take some cool pictures and video with it. The hut was right by a part of the road that had been “washed away” by an avalanche. It was a little too rough to ride so we ended up walking that section. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/YvVsfos.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There was another section called tooth breaker that was steep and known for people braking too hard and flying over their handlebars and breaking their teeth. I didn’t think it was that steep to be honest, but it was still fun to ride. Throughout the whole day Thomas ended up popping 3 tires and our guide Nell popped one also!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/9ZZv8Hk.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We finally made it to the very end of the road around 3pm and celebrated. Definitely sore wrists and forearms from bouncing around on the bikes. Nel told us some of the crazy stories he’s had while giving tours in the death road. Afterwards they drove us to a nearby hotel called Dos Rios for a “buffet” lunch, however it wasn’t really buffet because the lady was super stingy with giving out the meat (chicken and beef). Overall the lunch was good   After lunch we made the long ~2.5 hour drive back to La Paz to get dropped off at our hotel. </p>
