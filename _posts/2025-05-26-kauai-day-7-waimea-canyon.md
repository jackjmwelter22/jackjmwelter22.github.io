<p>Today we got up early at 6am, we ate some breakfast in the condo. We got packed in the car and left around 7 to drive west to the Waimea canyon. We were hiking the Awa’awapuhi trail, which was about 1 hour and 20 minutes drive.</p>

We ended up taking a lot of pictures and flying the drone around (being careful not to fly when the helicopters were around). </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stayed on the ridge and enjoyed the view while we ate a snack. We hadn’t seen a single person on the trail so we had the hike and the view completely to ourselves!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the snack we started the hike back up to the car. The hike back up was more strenuous than going down of course but the footing was easier going up. It ended up taking us about an hour and a half to get back up to the car. On the way back we passed by at least a dozen different people and the trailhead lot was fairly full of cars. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After doing the best to get the mud off our shoes we got in the car and drove further up the road to the Kalalau lookout. The lookout was pretty packed with people. The clouds were also a bit overcast so it was hard to see much. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>We didn’t spend much time there before getting back in the car. Since we were already all the way down the road we figured we might as well drive to the end which is where the start of the alakai trail is. However the road was closed right after the viewpoint which would have added an extra 2 miles to the alakai trail (glad we didn’t choose to hike that one!). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Next we drove to the Pu’u Hinahina Lookout. There wasn’t any parking so we created a spot, we ended up only staying for a quick picture at the lookout and then drove back down the road a few blocks to park and start the cliff lookout trail that goes to the waipo’o falls. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>We made the short hike back to the car and the. Started the drive back down to the coast. On the way down we stopped at two more viewpoints. The first viewpoint was the Pu'u Ka Pele Lookout. It was a quick pull off in the side of the road that had views looking back across the canyon to the cliff we were just hiking and the falls that we hiked above. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next stop was called the Waimea Canyon Lookout. This was a much bigger stop with lots of people. The view was pretty similar to the last stop. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>Unfortunately a massive tour bus left just before us so we were stuck following it slowly most of the way back down. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we got back to the coast we decided to stop in port Allen at the Kauai Island Brewing Company. The food was good I had the grilled chicken sandwich and Becca had a pulled pork bowl over rice. I also tried one of their hazy IPAs called pillow talk that was also really good. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://i.imgur.com/bvhzscA.jpeg","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"embed-handler","responsive":true} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we drove over to the nearby glass beach. A glass beach is exactly what it sounds like a beach with a bunch of glass. They actually call it sea glass, and the glass comes from discarded glass trash which overtime got broken up and smoothed into little glass pebbles and washed up on the beach. It’s actually really hard to tell there’s glass on the beach unless you look closely. Most of the glass is brown but there are a lot of other colors as well. There are only a handful of glass beaches in the world. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was interesting to see but after staying for a few minutes and taking some pictures we hopped back into the car and drove back to the condo. </p>
