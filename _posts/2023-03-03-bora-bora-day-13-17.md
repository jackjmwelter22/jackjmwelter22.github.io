<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We left Christchurch for Tahiti. We got to the airport early and left at 12 for a 1.5 hour flight to Auckland. In Auckland we had to walk pretty far to get to the international terminal and then go through security and everything again which was a pain. We made it to the gate about 10 minutes before boarding. We had some extra NZ cash so I got some food before the flight. There was nothing but McDonald’s close so I ended up getting a Big Mac (it’s been years since I’ve had McDonald’s). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The flight to Tahiti took about 5 hours. Becca and I had no one in between us which was nice! However we did have some crazies sitting behind us. Right when we got up to cruising altitude I reclined my chair, as I always do, and moments later the person behind me tapped my shoulder and asked me to not recline my chair. I politely told her no I wanted to recline. Throughout the flight they kept kicking our chairs and eventually culminating in the guy getting up to go to the bathroom and forcefully slamming my chair forward. Needless to say I was in shock, I let the chief flight attendant know who unfortunately decided not to do anything since there was only an hour left in the flight. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We arrived in Papeete around 8:30pm the day before we took off (we time travelled since French polynesia is 23 hours behind NZ). We got a shuttle to the nearby Hilton hotel. We stayed there for the night. The hotel looked really nice actually! Unfortunately we had an early flight so we didn’t get a chance to enjoy it. </p>

<p>Day 16</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day 3 in Bora Bora we started again with a really big brunch. Then we got picked up at the dock for our scuba diving trip! Our first dive was inside the lagoon area at a cleaning station for eagle rays. This was probably the coolest dive I've ever done we saw at least a dozen eagle rays (basically massive sting rays) that passed by us within an arms reach, they were sooo close! Unfortunately we didn't have any underwater cameras with us :( but the experience was amazing. Our second dive was just outside the lagoon and was nearly as cool as the first. This dive was saw dozens of white tipped sharks. At one point there was probably 40 of them, the reason is the snorkel boats will feed them which, needless to say, draws a ton of them over!</p>
