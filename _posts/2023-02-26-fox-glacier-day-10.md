<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today was an early day, we woke up at 4a and had a 3 hour drive to get to the town of fox glacier for our tour. The drive was dark most of the way which is apparently a bad time to drive as I had at least 4 rabbits run out in front of me :(. We ended up making it in early, around 7am. We waited in the parking lot for a bit before going into the tour shop and checking in around 7:45. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We met our guide Hector (from Brazil) and got suited up with our climbing boots and cold gear. Our tour was a glacier ice climbing tour! Becca and I did ice climbing once before in Bolivia and really enjoyed it (though we didn’t get to do much climbing). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we were geared up we shuttled a short distance to the helipad and took the quick 5min flight up to the fox glacier (I tracked it on ForeFlight). The Heli flight was cool but definitely not as cool as the one we took in Milford sound. You can tell they really shuttle people back and forth to this glacier. They had a mini helipad set up and helicopters landing every few minutes. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There are 3 main glaciers in the southern alps - mt cook, fox, and Franz Josef. The reason we actually ended up choosing fox glacier was because it was the only one we could get a ice climbing tour on the day we would be there. The other only had the Heli hikes which were clearly more common/popular because they cater to a wider audience (less physically demanding). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we arrived on the glacier we got our crampons on and then started hiking on the glacier. There were tons of crevasses some were super deep. We walked a short distance to a small incline wall to learn some fundamentals and practice our crampon work before walking another 15 minutes or so to a bigger ice wall. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/RwCGEQe.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once at the wall we practiced some drills with our ice picks while hector set up the top ropes for our climbing. Hector took a fairly long time to get everything set up. Once the lines were up hector checked us all out on belaying and then let us climb!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We started on the easier walls (less vertical) the moved to the more difficult routes (with small overhang). We did quite a few climbs before taking a break and eating our lunches. </p>

<p>We ate while hector packed up the lines and then we walked further down the glacier to a super large crevasse. hector was super overly precautious the whole trip and took forever to set everything up, he even put up fixed lines to cross a small crevasse. Once he was finally set up he lowered us down into the crevasse, it must’ve been 100 feet and then let us climb back up out of the canyon. </p>

<p>It was really cool, I did it twice and everyone else did it once. After climbing the crevasse we made our way back to the helipad and made the quick flight back to fox glacier. We took the bus back to the tour office and took off all our gear. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After leaving the tour office we drove a short distance to hike to lake matheisen, we didn’t do the full loop, we decided when we got to the lake to turn back because we were hungry. We stopped at a restaurant in town called Betsy jane (recommended by our guide). The food was really good! I had the ribeye. </p>
