<p>Today I woke up early, around 5:30 (it’s two hours behind Savannah here so I was expecting it). We packed up and had the buffet breakfast downstairs at the hotel restaurant. It was pretty good a bunch of omelets, fruit, waffles, etc. After breakfast we drove just north west of the city to the San Salvador volcano which is in El Boqueron park. The park itself was tiny, we walked through the whole thing in like 20 min. They had a bunch of cool views along the ridge of the crater looking down into the caldera. </p>

<p>There was another trail that left the park and went around the rim of the crater. It was technically closed (no idea why) but since we walked through the park so quickly we decided to walk around the little fence and take the trail. The trail went about a mile and a half around the crater with more views looking into the volcano. There was another path you could take to go down into the crater, but we decided against it because it looked like a long way down. On the trail we ran into a few people carrying supplies on their heads (no idea where they were going) along with some dogs and horses. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After reaching the other side of the rim we decided to turn around because the trail go significantly worse (very tight with lots of brush). When we got back to the car we changed into our swimsuits and drove to los chirros (a nearby spring). Unfortunately the spring was closed (even though everything online indicated otherwise) so we ended up continuing on to one of the Mayan ruins called Joya de Ceren. When we arrived that too was closed! At this point we were getting frustrated! We drove to our next stop, San Andres (another Mayan site) and luckily that was open. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>San Andres was a relatively small site consisting of some pyramids that had recently been been unearthed (they were covered after the eruption of a volcano). The site was where the Mayans would perform various ceremonies including human sacrifice! Some of the ceremonies they continue today in order to preserve the history of the Mayans. At least I’m pretty sure that’s what our tour guide said, the whole tour by was in Spanish!  It was surprising to me how little English is spoken here compared to some of the other Central American countries I’ve been too, my guess is that it’s due to less tourism. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tour lasted about 45 min. Afterwards we drove up to our next hotel at the lago de coatepeque. There was a big accident with two trucks on the highway, that caused a massive traffic jam on our way to the hotel. As a side note google maps is pretty useless here, the times we used it it tried to take us on non existent roads or way out of our way. When we arrived at the lake we checked in and unpacked then went to the dock for a snack. We tried the ceviche, which was okay. After eating we took the kayak out on the lake and went around the mini island in the lake. There were some really nice houses on the lake, we saw two helicopters take off from their lake houses on the island while we were kayaking. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>We got back to the hotel after about an hour and hung out on the dock and at the pool. There were a few other American tourists (with families in El Salvador) staying here so we talked with them. We had dinner at the hotel, I had a burger and becca ate more papusas (she really likes them haha). The burger was pretty sad, tomorrow we’re going to try eating somewhere away from the hotel to see if the food is better. </p>
