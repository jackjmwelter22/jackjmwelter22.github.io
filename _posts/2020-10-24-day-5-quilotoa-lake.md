<p>Today we woke up early, around 5:45, to head to Quilotoa lake. Google said the drive was just under 3 hours and it surprisingly was! These roads were the best we’ve seen so far. Quilotoa is in the Ecuadorian highlands, so there were tons of twists and turn. One interesting thing we saw was tons of dogs along the side of the road, I mean, there was at least 50 different dogs we saw along the way. We finally arrived and had to pay the entrance fee of $4 (2 per person) they didn’t have change so we ended up paying $5. We drove into the little town hoping to find a restaurant and bathroom. The first few places we tried were all closed and didn’t open until 10 (it was about 8:45). Finally after driving a little further we found an open restaurant. Apparently they only had one option for breakfast, scrambled eggs and bread with cheese and coffee or hot chocolate. The meal was only $4 for both of us and we only had a $20...again they had no change. Feeling like this was some sort of village scam I told the girl in my broken Spanish that we’d be back for lunch so that we would get our money’s worth haha. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Quilotoa lake, I think it’s technically a lagoon, is a big lake inside the crater of and old volcano. It looked absolutely stunning. Becca and I did the loop around the rim of the entire crater which was over 6 miles and thousands of feet in elevation gain and loss. Of course along the way we stopped and took tons of cool pictures. The whole hike around took about 4 hours. </p>

<p>Once we got back to our car we got a quick snack and decided to take the trail down to the lake itself. On the trail we made friends with a pup who showed us the way. </p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_7084.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1744" width="4032" height="3024"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the bottom we checked out the water, it was cold, and took more pictures. Instead of hiking all the way back up we decided to take the burro (donkey) ride. Becca had never ridden one so it was a fun experience, and of course more views. What I was super impressed with was the little girl who ran next to the donkey encouraging it to keep going. She ran up the trail like it was nothing!</p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_0933.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1738" width="3088" height="2320"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_4519.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1743" width="3088" height="2320">Back at the top we made our way back to the restaurant for lunch. Becca got the chicken and I got the guinea pig. I had tried it once before in Peru and it was pretty good so I figured why not, it was only $4...big mistake it was nothing like I remembered, super salty and rubbery. It must’ve been cooked different or something. Anyway after we ate the girl said she had my change and gave me $2, I guess it wasn’t a scam after all! Another weird thing is that $1 bills are not a thing, the exclusively use $1 and $0.5 coins here. </p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_0934.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1739" width="3024" height="4032"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got back in the car and made our way to our next stop, Cotopaxi. We were staying in a place called the secret garden that Google said was just under 3 hours away. The roads there were great, finally straight highways....until they weren’t...literally the last hour was the worst roads I had ever seen honestly I’m impressed our little car made it 😂. Of course the sun set when we got to the worst part. It was basically like driving a car up and Rocky Mountain with potholes everywhere. I drove in first gear literally the whole time. Nevertheless we finally made it just after 7. Dinner was included, chicken fajitas with rice and guac, it was awesome! We also met some people from the uk (one of them is hiking Cotopaxi with us) and some Ecuadorians. Everyone was really cool and friendly. There were also a bunch of pups that live here and they were all snuggled up together by the fire, super cute!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After dinner the host, Alejandro, showed us too our little room and around the property. After we settled in we went up to the jacuzzi for a half hour and then went back and got ready for bed. </p>
