Today we woke up just before 7 to gather our stuff and meet our driver outside to take us to the Allenby bridge border crossing. The drive took about an hour. Once there we had to go and pay the exit tax and go through passport control then get on a bus that drives you though "no-mans land" into Israel.

<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_2932.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1431" width="3024" height="4032">

When we arrived in Israel we had to go through passport control and security again. There was a food shop in the building so we used the rest of our Jordanian money on falafel sandwiches and snacks. The interrogation at passport control was no joke, they talked to me for a good 5 min asking me all sorts of questions about the trip, my life, job, family, etc. once we made it through we were picked up on the other side by our Israeli driver.

<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_2933.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1432" width="3088" height="2320">

It was probably another hour drive from the border, which is technically Palestine (I'm still not sure how that whole situation works), to Jafa gate of the old city of Jerusalem. There was a big Jewish festival going on today so we had to get dropped off a little farther from the gate. It was only a 10 min walk to the hostel from where we were dropped off. After dropping off our stuff we checked out the small rooftop of the hostel which had a really nice view of the old city. We left the hostel shortly after to go see the tower of David which is literally across the street from our hostel.

The tower had a free English tour so we decided to join it. I'm usually not much for tours, but the tour guide was awesome! He explained the crazy history of the city and why it's so important to Christians, Jews, and Muslims. He explained all the different civilizations that occupied Jerusalem. One funny thing is that the tower of David wasn't actually the tower of David, it was built by someone else but people consistently mistook it for the tower of David.<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_3480.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1411" width="2576" height="1920">

We got to go on top of one of the towers in the citadel which had an awesome view of the whole city. The guide pointed out all the important buildings.

<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_3482.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1413" width="2576" height="1920">

We also got to see the old prison where archeologists discovered tons of different history buried beneath the structure.

<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_2936.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1433" width="3024" height="4032">

After the tour we walked to western wall. It was super crowded with tons of people. We wrote down some prayers on pieces of paper and as tradition we went to the wall and put the paper in between the cracks in the stones.

<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_3484.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1415" width="2576" height="1920">

Next we made our way to a restaurant called Basti for lunch, I had shawarma that was delicious. Shawarma is basically a gyro except it's chicken instead of lamb. Walking the streets here is like walking through a maze. Everything is enclosed and there are shops literally everywhere. It's really cool to see. Also the military presence here is very noticeable. We've seen dozens of guards armed with automatic rifle patrolling the streets. We even noticed the guards arresting someone right outside the restaurant we were eating at.<img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/img_2938.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1434" width="4032" height="3024">

Once we finished lunch we decided to walk the Via Dolorosa aka the stations of the cross. The path is relatively short and marks out where Jesus fell and all the other stations. It finally ends at the church of sepulchre which was built on top of where Jesus was crucified and buried. It was really cool to see the path and the spots Jesus went. Inside the church we had to wait in a crazy long line to see the burial tomb. The church is considered to be the holiest site in Christianity. There was a priest in front of the tomb that was essentially a bouncer and had to kick people out of the tomb on a few occasions because they didn't follow the rules or were taking too long.

After seeing the church we walked back to the hostel to rest for a bit. We watched a simpsons episode on Jerusalem that our guide had recommended as funny and also informative.

For dinner we walked about a mile to the 'newer' side of Jerusalem to an area called the shuk. The streets on the way there were crazy crowded for the Jewish holiday. We went to a place called the beer bazar and had some good beers. While we were there we met some locals who gave us some food recommendations. After beer we walked further down the shuk for dinner. I ended up getting a chicken sandwich. After dinner we made our way back to the hostel. On the way I stopped for a gelato :)
