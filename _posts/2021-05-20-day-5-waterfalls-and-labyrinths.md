<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up around 7 and had breakfast at the house we were staying in. Dora (the host) cooked us a typical breakfast of eggs, beans, bread, cheese, and fried plantain. The bread was super stale but everything else was good! Becca wasn’t feeling so great (as you might be able to tell from her expression), maybe Mexican wasn’t the best idea last night!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1942,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/img_1276.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-1942" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After breakfast we got ready and took the on site coffee tour. We were pleasantly surprised to find out that we finally had a guide, Hector, who spoke English! He took us through the whole coffee making process from picking the coffee plants (done by hand) to processing, drying, sorting (done by hand), and roasting. It was crazy to see all the people they had sorting out all the coffee beans (looking for beans with imperfections such as broken or miscolored). It was also really cool that the coffee plant still used the same machines from the 1930s, the year they started producing coffee. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1941,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/img_1278.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-1941" /><figcaption>Machines and drying patio</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1961,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was also really cool to see the warehouse where they stored all the packaged coffee beans (this was not very full according to hector, he said usually the stacks almost make it to the ceiling. This coffee plant is medium sized and does about 10 million pounds of coffee per season. They process a mix of their own beans as well as beans from other farms in the cooperative. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1956,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/img_5230.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-1956" /><figcaption>Coffee on coffee</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the end of the tour we got to try some of the coffee. I’m not much of a coffee guy so I’ll refrain from critiquing but Becca liked it and got a bag to bring back home. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the coffee tour we drove down what’s known as the ruta de las Flores about 20 min to the town of Juayua (pronounced “why-you-ahh”). There we found another tour company with a guide (Eduardo) that was willing to take us on a tour of the siete casacadas (7 waterfalls). Amazingly Eduardo also spoke good English. We ended up driving to the start of the trail in order to save a few hours of walking. To get to the start of the trail was pretty rough, we drove for about 15min into what seemed like the middle of the Forrest. At this point we were getting skeptical that this wasn’t some sort of ruse, but Eduardo seemed trustworthy and he was tiny so I figured I could take him if I had to :P </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The start of the hike was basically all down hill and crossing a few rivers until we arrived at the first waterfall. </p>

<p>Luckily the trail was a loop so we didn’t have to climb back down any of the falls. After returning to the car Eduardo took us to a place for lunch called cafe Goya. The food there was really good and super inexpensive (it was $10 for the 3 of us). We all had the chicken with vegetables and rice. At the restaurant we watched some of the sand soccer game which apparently is really popular here. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1965,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/img_5295.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-1965" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we dropped Eduardo back off at the tour office and continued back to another town on the route called Apeneca our first stop was the labyrinth. We really weren’t expecting much but it was actually super cool! We spent about 15 minutes finding our way to the center to ring the bell and then explored the rest of the labyrinth. </p>

<p>Afterwards we decided to drive up the mountain behind the town to a place called laguna verde (green lagoon). It was recommended as a cool place by the police officer on our Santa Ana hike so we figured we’d give it a shot. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The lagoon turned out to be really disappointing, it basically looked like a homeless encampment with kids playing with trash in the murky water of the lagoon. We took a quick picture and quickly headed back to town. A guy came up as we were leaving to try and get us to pay a “fee” but I told him all we had was a credit card (which of course the didn’t accept) so he let us leave, after all we were only there for like 2 minutes. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After exploring Apeneca, we made our way back to Ataco and parked at the main park to explore the city. There are tons of cool murals painted on the walls of all the buildings here. We ended up stopping at a coffee shop called Brewers, I had a delicious snickers milkshake. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":1950,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/img_1292.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-1950" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Afterwards we made our way up the hill and the northern end of town and found another labyrinth, this one was smaller and only cost $1 so we figured might as well. it ended up actually being more tricky than the large one we did. After about 15min we escaped and went to dinner at the next door restaurant which was on the rooftop with great views of the city below. We also saw a peacock roaming around the grounds. </p>
