<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up around 5:30 am again, thanks Bennett ;). Unfortunately breakfast didn't open until 7am,  so we hung out in the room and packed up to go.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Breakfast was poolside at a table just outside our room. They had a menu (instead of a buffet). I had the American breakfast which consisted of eggs, bacon, toast, and fruit. It was pretty good. Bennett had the pancakes and Becca had the Nicaraguan tradicional which was eggs, fried plantains, rice &amp; beans, and fruit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Everything was good. After eating we finished packing and checked out of the hotel. I went around to get the car and we loaded up. This drive was about an hour and a half to the town of San Jorge, which is a main port on the lake for the Ometepe ferry.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The drive was smooth though we did get “stopped” twice by female police officers to check our documents. I’m not sure if I was driving too fast or something (probably) but they didn’t stop any of the cars behind me. We got to the port just after 9. A man there helped us figure out how to get all the tickets and everything for the car, he seemed sketchy at first, but he never asked for money or anything from us. It was way easier than I thought it would be. Overall it was around 800 cordoba for everything.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After we got the tickets we got our car lined up for the ferry. About 10 am (30 minutes before) they had us load the cars onto the ferry. We ended up parking with the passenger side super close to the wall of the boat so Becca had to climb over the car seat to get out!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>The ferry left right at 10:30 am (surprisingly prompt for Nicaragua). The ride was really smooth and the boat was relatively empty so there was a lot of space. </p>

<p>The ride took about an hour. Getting off was easy and we decided to stop for lunch at a nearby restaurant called Comedor Esmeralda. I had a burrito that was really good and big.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Some random dudes, Gustavo and Allen, came up to us and chatted. They were really friendly (definitely drunk) and of course one was trying to promote his tour company lol. Also of course the cook and waitress loved Bennett. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we drove about 50 minutes to our hotel, Totoco Eco-Lodge. On the way we had to drive across the literal runway of the island airport which was pretty cool. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We had read reviews that the road up to our hotel was bad and you needed a 4x4 suv to make it - that was not at all the case. The road was mostly paved the whole way- a little rocky and steep but overall not bad.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our hotel was... interesting. Any place with “Eco” in the name should probably be a big red flag. Overall the place was nice- beautiful views and friendly staff. We were disappointed the pool was pretty dirty with algae- Bennett was especially disappointed he didn’t get to swim. There was also a minor ant infestation in our bathroom…and no air conditioning.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
