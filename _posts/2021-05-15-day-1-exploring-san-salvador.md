<p>Let’s start with day 0 (yesterday). I got a call from the rental car company...I knew it wouldn’t be good...my assumption was correct, they had no more cars for us tomorrow, I seriously couldn’t believe it happened again (last time was our Ecuador car rental)! Luckily it wasn’t right before we were boarding the airplane this time! He called back a few minutes later saying he found a car! We finished up packing and getting ready. Becca’s sister and brother-in-law came over (they’re staying and watching the pups for us) we made some creme brûlée with the new torch, it was delish!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next day we woke up early around 6am. We finished packing, ate some breakfast and made our way to the airport around 6:30. Our first flight was to DC and then onto San Salvador. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We landed at 1:30 which was a little early. Getting through passport control/customs was easy, they just checked our vaccination cards. I had tried to make two other back up rental car reservations, just in case the other fell though, and also because we had to pick that one up in the city (40min from the airport). We tried our luck with the Avis rental car stand at the airport. Of course they “didn’t have the car I reserved” but had another one that was more expensive. Luckily a local person was behind and helped us negotiate the price back down to what it was supposed to be. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we got the car it was an easy drive to the city. The roads were surprisingly nice. The hotel was even nicer, after checking in we took an Uber to the centero historico of the city. Once there we visited a couple different churches (all of which had masses going on) and took a tour of the national palace.  Lot of people were staring at us as we walked around, I’m not sure if it’s because we’re both tall or because they don’t get a lot of American tourists haha. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We learned a little bit about the history of el Salvador. Apparently it was the most prosperous country of Central America until the 1980s, when their civil war occurred, which didn’t get resolved until 1992. The palace had a bunch of different rooms, all of them were painted a different color and named accordingly (I.e the blue room, yellow room, etc). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After touring the palace we walked around the squares and open air markets. There were tons of people! Everyone was wearing masks even outdoors (it was super hot and humid). We grabbed some pupsas (the National dish) for a street vendor as a snack. After walking around we took another Uber to a restaurant called tapioca margoth, I read online that they had really good pupusas. We had 6 different types of pupusas, horchata, and a fried plantain for desert. A pupusa is a thick corn tortilla filled with different combinations of cheese, meat, beans. Its the national dish of El Salvador. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>After dinner we got a ride to the nearby brewery called Cadejo. The beer was good, after a few drinks we went back to the hotel. We went and checked out the pool of our hotel but it was a little cold so we didn’t stay long. There was also a super fancy wedding going on right next to the pool. Afterwards we relaxed and went to bed. </p>
