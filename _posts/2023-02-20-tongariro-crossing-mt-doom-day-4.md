<p>Today we woke up around 5 to eat and get ready for our big hike. We left the apartment at 5:45 and drove to the north end of the tongariro national park to meet our shuttle. How it works is everyone parks at the north end of the park and then takes a shuttle to the south starting point of the hike and through hikes back to their car. The hike is about 12 miles from start to finish with some optional summits, which of course I did. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once on the bus the ride to the starting point took about 30 min so we started hiking right about 7am. The one problem with this shuttle system is it causes a ton of people to start at the exact same time (there’s only one shuttle time option) so the trail gets filled with tons of people and no one is spread out in the beginning. We started with a pretty good pace which got us away from most of the crowds. Initially the weather was foggy/cloudy but it quickly burnt off as the sun rose. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>After about 4 miles into the hike we hit the split for the first summit option. The first summit was Mt Ngauruhoe (aka mt doom from lord of the rings). Of course a summit with such a menacing name would not be an easy hike. Somehow I managed to convince Pat to make a go at it with me while the girls waited for us. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/5grEBWi.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The start was steep and only got steeper as we went up. This was compounded with the fact that there was no path and the mountainside was basically all scree/shale (loose rock) so it was essentially like climbing up a massive sand dune…super tiring. After some climbing we found a more rigid section of rock with more stable footing that made the ascent easier, more of a scramble/climb. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>After about an hour Pat couldn’t take it anymore so he decided to wait while I made the last push to the summit. The top segment was not quite as steep and was much easier to hike. I ended up pulling out the drone and taking some epic shots at the summit before making my way back down. The coolest thing (besides the views) was I was the only person up there!</p>
