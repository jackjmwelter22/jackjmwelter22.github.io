<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up at 8:20 and had breakfast at 8:30. It was similar to the previous day with a ton of pastries and an egg omelette. After eating we got ready and met in the lobby at 9 for our walking tour of Fes. Our tour guide was Jamal, who is from just outside of Fes, but randomly lived in Wisconsin so his English was really good and he had a tonnn of facts about the city and it’s history that he shared with us so I’ll try to include the few I remember. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For starters the medina was built to be economically diverse, the king did not want separate neighborhoods for rich and poor so they were mixed together. And in order to prevent people from flaunting their wealth no windows were allowed to the outside and no decorations, also when you opened the door to your house there must be a wall so that others can’t see inside so from the outside it’s impossible to tell the size of the house and this also makes all the streets/alleys (over 9,000 of them) look very similar making it very easy to get lost! There are 3 types of homes depending on size, a dar (smallest), a Riad, and a palace. Though the dars that we saw still looked huge!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After talking a bit in the lobby we went to the van and made a quick stop at the kings palace. There are 7 gold gates for the main entrance of the palace, but they’re really more for show. we also saw the old nearby Jewish street where they’ve been filming the new indiana Jones movie. This considered the moroccan bourbon street, this is outside of the Medina, hence all the windows. </p>

<p>Our next stop was up one of the nearby hills to a fort that had a nice panoramic view overlooking the city and the Medina. The medina is split into two parts by the river. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next stop was a ceramic shop where they showed us how they make dishes and bowls out of clay. They also make clay tiles which they intricately cut, color, and create mosaics with. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2268,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_0403.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-2268" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Finally we made it to the Medina, our guide took us through the tight winding streets to show us a bunch of different districts of the Medina. There are no motorized vehicles in the Médina because of the small streets, so only donkeys and mules are used to bring stuff in and out. The districts are divided by what they produce, there’s a weaver district, carpentry, dying, jewelry, and tannery among others. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2269,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_0408.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-2269" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The first main area we walked through was the market which had all sorts of unique foods. They basically sell every part of an animal you could think of. They had a “Moroccan KFC” where you pick out which live chicken you want and they slaughter it and prepare it in front of you. They also sold goats and even camel meat!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2270,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After walking through the market we passed through the dying district and then went to a carpet co-op, this place was filled with all sorts of rugs it was pretty impressive. They also showed a demonstration of how they made the rugs. Of course after they tried to sell us rugs, no of us were interested but Alyssa was too nice and they really tried to get her to buy one. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2271,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<p>After the rug shop we stopped to eat at another dar that was converted into a restaurant, the food was really pricey and it seemed like they took all the tourists here. I had the chicken pastilla, which was like a sugary pastry with meat inside. It was good but a little too sweet for me. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After lunch we walked to the weaver district. The girls were interested in getting scarfs which they had loads of. The shop keeper also tried to convince me to get one by showing how it could be used to make a cool turban 😂.  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2273,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After getting the scarfs we walked to a madrassa, this is basically a religious high school with a mosque in the back. In order to get in you need to memorize the entire Quran! This madrasa used to be a trash dump actually but the prince decided to convert it into a beautiful madrasa. This was done to prove to people that “you can turn  garbage dump into a beautiful mosque” (a common saying in Morocco). The reason he did this was because he had married a prostitute for one of his wives and wanted to convince people she was beautiful despite her past. Also randomly our guide also told us an in detail story about his circumcision when he was 5….it was really strange and not sure how it came up I guess it was something about the culture😂.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2274,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the madrasa we walked to our last stop, the tannery. The tannery is just outside of the Médina. When you walk in they give you mint leaves because the smell is pretty bad (they use pigeon droppings for the ammoniac acid to clean the hides)  after a while though you get used to it and it’s not that bad. They had all sorts of leather goods, apparently goat leather is the best type. We didn’t buy anything but of course they had us try on stuff and tried to sell us. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the tannery we got back on the bus to make our way back to our palace hotel. We rested for about half an hour before heading out to the super market to get water and snacks for the next couple days. After that we went to a nearby restaurant our guide recommended called cinema diner. The food was decent, I tried the camel burger which really just tasted like a normal burger and I also had a milkshake which was pretty bad (it was really liquidy). After eating we drove back to our hotel and hang out on the balcony before going to bed around 10. </p>
