<p style="text-align:justify;">Buckle up this is a long one. (Sorry in advance for all the rants, but not really)</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">This morning we woke up at 4:45 in order to check out of our hostel in Beijing and have enough time to make it to our flight at 8:30. What we failed to realize was that the metro doesn't even open until 5:45! ...Rookie mistake. We definitely could've slept almost an extra hour. To kill time we wandered around and found a local grocery store and picked up some breakfast snacks to hold us over. We got on the metro right as it opened at 5:45 and finally were able to get a seat! (Usually the metro is super packed). The metro took us to the train station which then took us to the airport. Since we didn't have our boarding passes we had to wait in the long line of people who have bags to check like a bunch of peasants (I hate waiting in meaningless lines). Even though we got a later start than we planned we still made it to our gate with plenty of time to spare.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">The flight from Beijing to Hong Kong took just over 3 hours, which normally wouldn't have been so bad, however no electronics were allowed to be used on the flight...at all...like for the whole flight. I ended up getting yelled at 3 times by the flight attendants to turn off my phone 😂. It was an honest mistake actually, the first time I thought they just wanted me to keep it turned off during taxi and takeoff. I started using my phone again when I thought we reached cruise altitude and got told yet again to turn it off. I figured I just turned it on too early and had to wait until we were at a higher cruising altitude or something. Finally the third time they sent their best English speaking flight attendant over to explain to me that no electronics of any kind are allowed to be on during a flight in china. I tried to explain how there's this mode you can use on the phone for airplanes but he said that still wasn't allowed 😂.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Anywaysss...after all that I gave up and decided just to watch the inflight movie they had playing. I'm not sure what the movie was called but essentially it's about this girl who is super sick at home because her immune system is so weak if she goes outside she'll die. It gets to the last ~20 minutes or so of the movie and then it decides to restart all the way back to the beginning! So I watch the whole beginning of the movie all over again and right when it's about to end then same thing happened! So I still don't know if this girl survives or not, very frustrating, avoid flying China Southern if you can help it!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">As we descended through the clouds on the final approach to HK you could see the massive amounts of sky scrapers that lined the shores of the city, it was really incredible.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Once we landed we quickly made our way out of the airport. On the way we were stopped by a nice lady who I originally thought was trying to sell us something but ended up giving us a map and a lot of good advice on how to get around and what to see (I'm already starting to like this place!). We took the bus to the nearby cable car station to head to Lantau island.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">The cable car trip over was really cool and we got to see some great views of the island and airport.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-468" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1174.jpg" width="3024" height="4032" /> Lantau really looked similar to Hawaii to me. Also there was a monk on our cable car which was neat 🤓. The cable car lets you off near the top of the mountain in a small village. In the village there were a bunch of cows/buffalo (in not really sure) so of course We had to get a selfie with one. Luckily they were friendly and only tried to lick us.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-469" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1180.jpg" width="1932" height="2576" /> One of the main attractions on Lantau island is a big Buddha that sits on the very peak which had stairs you could hike up to. The hike was a struggle with our already sore legs and blistered feet. Jamie and I made our way to the top just in time to get some good pictures before a cloud passed over and made the whole area foggy.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-473" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1185.jpg" width="2576" height="1932" /><img class="wp-image-472" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1188.jpg" width="2576" height="1932" /><img class="wp-image-471" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1196.jpg" width="1932" height="2576" /> Also nearby on the island was a Buddhist temple that we stopped to see. Honestly it looked like all the other temples, there wasn't anything special about it that I could tell.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-474" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1197.jpg" width="2576" height="1932" /> After the temple we took the cable car back down. In the cable car we met an Australian and English couple that were super friendly and gave us some advice on things to see in Hong Kong and also recommended going to Macau (a nearby island) for the day, which we plan to do tomorrow.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">The cable car dropped us off right by the metro which we took over too Kowloon (the mainland portion of HK). Our hostel had a metro stop right next to it which was super convenient. Our hostel was tucked away on the 14th floor of a building right in the middle of downtown, couldn't have picked a better location!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Some things I noticed about Hong Kong is that it's very westernized, they have a lot of the same restaurant chains you'd see in the US and also 7-11s are literally everywhere (I think they maybe went bankrupt in the US? But they are thriving in HK!). Hong Kong was a British colony at one point. So they drive on the wrong side (left side) of the road and all their cars have the steering wheel on the right. The also walk on the left side which led to some awkward moments trying to move around people on the sidewalk. Oh and one last thing, there is a ton of money in HK, basically every car you see is $70k or more. We saw at least 2 Bentleys, Rolls Royce, if you drove a Mercedes you were poor.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Back to the story, after checking in and dropping off our bags we made our way to the ferry in order to take it across the bay (a 10 min trip) to get to the island of Hong Kong. We got on the ferry just as it was pulling away from the dock. The trip over gave us a great view of the skyline.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-476" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1200.jpg" width="4032" height="3024" /> We we landed on the other side we quickly realized we went to the wrong port! It wasn't a big deal just added an extra mile of walking. You'd think a mile wouldn't be bad right? Wrong. Apparently whoever designed the city hated pedestrians and didn't want people waking around. The place was a fucking maze! Some streets had side walks others didn't. There was construction everywhere (they don't have enough sky scrapers apparently) which definitely didn't help. Because of all the buildings the GPS signal was about as useful as an inflatable dart board. After running around in circles we finally made it to our destination, the peak tram. This was a tram that was going to take us up to Victoria's peak (the highest in HK) to give us a gray view of the city. At this point it was around 6pm and it was packed with people and the line was wrapping around the building. If there's one thing I hate more than lines it's crowds of awful people! Everyone was pushing and trying to cut in front of us... so much fun. After about an hour we finally made it on the tram (in hindsight we should've just hiked up and not had to deal with all the people).</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-478" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1219.jpg" width="4032" height="3024" /> The tram took us to the viewing deck which again was packed with the worst kind of people (more pushing and shoving). We were able to squeeze in and get a few really cool pictures of the skyline all lit up with flashing lights, it was really incredible.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-480" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1216.jpg" width="2576" height="1932" /><img class="wp-image-479" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1226.jpg" width="2560" height="1918" /> We tried to get out of the observation building but it took forever to find the exit, I was super hangry at this point so my patience was running low. After ~20min we made it out and found a nearby restaurant called Maks Noodles. I got the beef wonton which really hit the spot and the prices were super cheap.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">We went to the team to make our way back down and the line was even bigger! 😡😡. There was an ice cream store next to the line(couldn't have picked a better location) so we got some to make the wait a little better. When we got back to the street level we decided to try this rooftop bar called ce la vie. Now when we checked in I specifically asked the receptionist guy if the bars had a dress code and he said no. Well of course we get to the rooftop and they tell us they can't let us in because they have a strict dress code policy! More frustration! 😂</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">We left the bar and walked to see the worlds longest escalator which was right by us. Honestly it was pretty lame, idk what I was expecting...it was just a really long escalator. The escalator dropped us off at Hollywood street which we heard was a cool street to walk around. I'm not sure if we were at the right part of the street but it didn't seem very cool!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">We decided to make our way back to the hostel. On the way we stopped at the 7-11 to get Jamie some shampoo (hers was confiscated at the airport 😂) and I got a beer, I needed one!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">At the hostel we got changed into some nicer clothes and decided to go to the Ozone bar, the highest bar in the world. We called ahead to make sure we knew he dress code! I had to wear pants and the walk there was about a mile and a half. I thought Savannah was humid but Hong Kong is just as bad if not worse! By the time we got there I was drenched! We took the elevator up 118 floors to get to the bar. It was an open air bar and had an awesome view of the HK skyline below. Not only was it the highest bar in the world but it also had the highest prices! 😂. Totally worth the experience though. They also had the nicest bathroom I've ever seen in a bar.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-481" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_1222.jpg" width="2576" height="1932" /> After having a beer and hanging out at the top we took the metro to avenue of stars which is supposed to be a cool path along the bay you can walk. The problem was it was under construction 😫 so we walked back to the hostel. Today was a super long and exhausting day! In total my phone says we walked over 14 miles! Tonight will be another early day as we plan to take the morning ferry over to Macau 🇲🇴.</p>
