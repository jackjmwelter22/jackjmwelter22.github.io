<p>We got up at ~6am after some much needed rest. It was surprisingly easy to switch time zones, probably because I was so exhausted. We ate a quick breakfast in the hotel and then loaded up to drive south to visit the waitomo glowworm caves (about 2 hrs away). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got to the meet point around 8:30 to check in and ate some snacks before being shuttled to the gate location to get our wetsuits and harnesses. </p>

<p>To start the tour, after some instruction, we abseiled (rappelled) off a platform about 100 feet under ground through a tiny hole, super fun! I’ve abseiled before but this time was with a unique device called a 5 bar break bar </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/Fk0lyvS.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the rappelling we made our way through the dark cave by zip lining, crawling, swimming, and even tubing! The really cool thing about the cave is the glow worms. Basically these little worms that grow all over the walls of the cave and glow light blue. Pictures didn’t do it Justice but it was really cool to see! Fun fact the glow is called bioluminescence and is actually their poo being processed 😂. Their light attracts bugs that they eat. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The full tour took about 5 hours to make it all the way through the cave. Overall we had a great time, our tour guides max and Astrid were awesome! I’d highly recommend the tour if you’re near Auckland. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the tour finished we had to rush to our car in order to get to our hobbiton tour on time. Hobbiton was ~1 hour away. Thankfully along the way we were able to call and shift our tour from the 3:10p to 3:30 which gave us plenty of time to make it there. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pat and Sarah got to Auckland in the morning so they weren’t able to make it to the cave tour with us but they were able meet us for the hobbiton tour. While we waited for our tour we had a small snack, muffins and a beef pot pie. There were some very friendly/daring birds that were happy to try and eat your food so you had to be careful!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>None of us are super huge lord of the rings fans, but since it was so close by we had to make the stop. The tour drives you from the entrance of the farm to the main village. There are something like 47 hobbit holes in the village. Interestingly some doors are small and others are large, they do this to create the illusion the hobbits are small while others (like wizards) are tall. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>I’m not sure how much money was spent putting the village together but it had to be a lot. The level of detail they went to was seriously impressive. There is a massive tree on top of bag end (bilbos house) that was built specifically for the movie, I guess they couldn’t find any other trees they liked so they built one. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After walking through the village we crossed the bridge and ended at the green dragon where we got a complimentary drink before getting back on the tour bus. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tour was really cool, definitely a zoo of tourists, but it seems like they have a fairly good system in place to shuttle everyone through. After the tour we drove another hour to Rotorua, a volcanic lake. We made a quick stop to drop off pat and Sarah’s car and then grabbed dinner at a small pizza place by the lake called capiazza. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/jQ8pVLL.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<p>The pizza was actually really good I had the meat lovers. One thing we’ve all noticed about New Zealand is that they seem to have all the American chains/brands, if it wasn’t for the accents and left had driving you’d think you were in the US. After eating we made our way to our Airbnb which literally was right on the lake and had an awesome view. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/5epw5Pj.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">The backyard</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After getting settled we decided to go to this thing called the redwoods night tree walk. Apparently New Zealand and California are the only two places in the world that have redwoods. The ones in New Zealand aren’t quite as big as they were only planted about 120 years ago during an experiment to see what types of trees would grow here. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The line for the tree walk was pretty ridiculous but we decided to wait it out. There walk itself was super cool - basically a bunch of suspension bridges between trees with lights and lasers lighting everything up. </p>
