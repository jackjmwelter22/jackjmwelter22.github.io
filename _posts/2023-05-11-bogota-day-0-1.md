<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I worked from home while Becca had the day off. We left Savannah around 3:30/4 and drove about 2 hours to Jacksonville airport. The flights were much better and cheaper from Jacksonville so it was worth it. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ended up getting to the airport really early. We used spothero to get a covered parking spot for $7/day at a place called Wally park. Security at the airport was super quick. We got to the gate early and were lucky enough to get an earlier flight out of Jacksonville to Miami at 6:30 instead of 8pm. However, we weren’t as lucky in Miami, instead of leaving at 10:30p our flight left closer to 11:30 which meant we didn’t arrive in Bogota until ~2am 😩. We  had a prearranged ride to our hostel which was super nice, we ended up getting to bed around 2:30. We are staying at the Cranky Croc hostel located in the historic central area of Bogota. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Even though we went to be really late I still woke up around 7am with a lot of energy. We ended up eating breakfast at the hostel at 8am. I had the Colombian breakfast and (even though I’m not a coffee drinker) a latte, both were really good!</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After breakfast we got ready for the day and then walked ~2 blocks to our first stop-  the museum of gold. We spent way too long trying to find the entrance (people were telling us different things!).  We finally figured it out and started exploring the museum around 9. The museum was pretty big, 3 stories and of course filled with all kinds of gold treasures and trinkets. Overall it was cool to see but wouldn’t recommend going out of your way for it, but if you’re in Bogota the entry fee is only $1/person so you might as well go. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got out of the gold museum after about an hour, just in time to meet up for our walking tour (which was conveniently meeting right outside of the museum).  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tour was really interesting but long! We walked around the city from 10a-1p. We learned a lot about Colombian history and some interesting stories, here’s my summary of the stops and most interesting things I took away. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The main reason for the city of Bogota was gold  exploration, Spanish explorers took a wrong turn on the river and ended up in Bogota. The higher altitude of Bogota lends itself to nice weather and no mosquitos making it a popular place to live. Colombia used to be one large country consisting of Ecuador, Venezuela, and Panama when it first split from Spain. However, after many power struggles the countries split up into what we know them today. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On the walking tour we walked around an area called La Candelaria. There is a ton of really cool graffiti in this city which has a story behind it. To make it short a kid who was graffitiing was shot and killed by the police which caused a lot of protest and then shortly after Justin Bieber (who was on tour) decided he wanted to graffiti a building and while doing so was being watched by the police in his entourage who didn’t  do anything to stop him. This led to the decriminalization of graffiti. Finally, to avoid their buildings being “tagged” by less talented graffiti “artists”, many building owners paid talented graffiti artists to graffiti their buildings. </p>

<p>We finished the tour at 1 and decided to stop at a restaurant that Becca heard about called La puerta falsa (the false door). Apparently Anthony bourdain ate here. We had the popular chocolate sanatferrano which is hot chocolate with cheese, it sounds weird but it’s actually really good! It came with almojábana which is a tapioca bread (that Becca can eat!) and we also split a tamal, a rice and chicken dish cooked in plantain leaves. The food was good and the restaurant was really unique, very tiny. </p>

<p>After eating we walked back to the museum of gold to catch our food tour (lots of tours today!). The first stop on the tour was a fruit stand where we tried 3 different fruits: sweet mango, mangosteen, and rambutan. </p>

<p>The next stop was on the way towards plaza bolivar at an empanada and arepas shop. We had the meat empanada (called empanada casera - empanada of the house) and the arepa con huevo (egg arepa) both of which were very good!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next stop was plaza bolivar where we had grilled corn on the cob, a traditional snack for colombianos. </p>

<p>Next spot was an indoor restaurant where we tried ajico - a chicken and potato soup, barbecue - one veal and the other capybara (a rodent), patacones - flattened and fried plantain, Colombian beer, and a coconut lemonade. All of it was really good, yes even the rat (the had a small stuffed capybara nearby so you could see exactly what you were eating 😂). I was most surprised by the coconut lemonade it was really good and tasted kind of like key lime pie, but better. </p>

<p>It started pouring rain while we were in the restaurant, lucky. We waited about 10 minutes before stopping at a street vendor to try a desert called oblesa. Basically two thin waffles sandwiched with your choice of filling, I went for the condensed milk and black berry our guide recommended. Very delicious!</p>

<p>Becca and I decided to end the tour early after this stop, the next stop was hot chocolate with cheese and then coffee which we already had tried. Instead we stopped at the Botero museum, he’s a famous artist who apparently really likes painting fat naked people. He even had a fat version of the Mona Lisa, super random! Botero donated a ton of his paintings to the government on the condition that the museum would be free to the public. </p>

<p>While the hostel worked on drying the room and Becca’s clothes, we walked up the hill to the teleférico to see Monserrat- a church on the mountain boarding bogota. You can hike up to Monserrate but Becca and I opted to take the cable car (~$6 each). The views at the top were awesome. I started to fly my drone but only got a few pictures before the security said I had to land, there weren’t any no droning signs around so I didn’t think it would be a problem. </p>
