<p>Today we got up at 6 and drove ~10 minutes to the airport and dropped off the rental car. We flew sun express which is a budget airline - the check in process was smooth, but our flight got delayed an hour. Once we were in the air the flight was a quick 50 minute hop to Kayseri airport. As a side note much of the farming in this area is done in greenhouses to protect the plants from the scorching sun, so you end up seeing towns full of greenhouses. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We had to wait for the rental agency again - this time a random guy met us in the parking lot and gave us the car - there was no office or anything. We got another fiat rental car which looked fine on the outside, however we very quickly found out that the air conditioning did not work at all which was unfortunate because it was really hot outside. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We made the ~hour drive to a stop just outside Goreme (the town where we were staying) at a place called the Zelve open air museum. It cost ~$10 per person to enter and was a path through The Valley with tons of buildings that were cut into the rock that you could explore. It used to be a monastery. They had stables for livestock, a winery, food storage cellars, a church, and housing. It was really cool we explored a bunch of the caves. I took my drone out to fly and got yelled at almost immediately so unfortunately no good shots, just one video. </p>

<p>The Zelve valley was an early settlement dating back thousands of years, and habitants carved homes into the volcanic rock. During the night and 13th century’s, Zelve became a monastic retreat for Christians and was a significant religious center. One of the caves we explored was a really big church. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the zelve museum we drove into town and checked into our hotel (at 2pm). We’re staying at the sora cave hotel, it’s up almost at the top of the hill in Goreme, and as you might have guessed,  built into the mountain as a cave. Really cool and the hotel manager is super friendly…almost too friendly lol. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After checking in we went to grab lunch at a nearby restaurant called the wooden spoon. The restaurant was also in a cave and you sat on cushions on the floor to eat. I had the meat pide (pizza) and Becca had a diced lamb dish. We also tried Aryan, a salty yoghurt drink, I guess it wasn’t bad but I wouldn’t order it again. The food was good though!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we went back to the hotel to rest for an hour. At four we went downtown to the bike shop to see if they had availability for mountain bikes this weekend. The guy there was super nice and they did have availability. Afterwards we went to a café called kings café, and had milkshakes. I had strawberry and Becca had mango. They’re both good but very sugary.</p>

<p>Afterwords, we went back to our hotel to rest until we went to Uchisar Castle (also built into rock) right before sunset. The castle was about 15 minutes away, and a little higher than Goerme and at the top had awesome views of the city and surrounding Valley’s. The Goerme vista was on the way to the castle so we made a quick stop there for a photo. </p>
