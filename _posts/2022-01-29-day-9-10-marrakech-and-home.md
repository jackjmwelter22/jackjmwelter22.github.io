<p>Today Becca and I woke up at 5:30 and got picked up for a hot air balloon tour! They picked up another group and drove us ~30 minutes outside the city to a camp where they gave us some tea/coffee while we watched them inflate the balloons. </p>

<p>The balloons were inflated and we all loaded up. Our basket was fairly large and held ~16 people comfortably. We got up in the air just as the sun was rising. You could see the Atlas Mountains landscape and other hot air balloons flying around. It was really fun!</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ended up flying for an hour and getting up to 1,150 meters (the max is 1,500 so you don’t hit any airplanes landing at the nearby airport). We landed in an open field just off the road. They actually were able to maneuver the balloon when we got close to the ground and land it onto the truck trailer which was pretty impressive. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After jumping out they took us back to the camp for a quick breakfast and then drove us back to the Medina. We were originally supposed to pay by credit card but the tour guide reallyyy wanted us to pay another way. We ended up trying PayPal and that didn’t work. Eventually I just wired the money and he paid the fee (it was a bit of a pain). We walked back to our riad to meet the rest of our group. We had a quick lunch and then Becca and I walked to the tannery district (not as impressive as Fes) and to one of the main Medina gates (east of us) then we met back up with the others north of the medina at the Jardín Majorelle. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The garden was just that basically a big garden, it was cool but surprisingly expensive for Morocco (~$13 US). We walked around for 45 min and took some pictures. The place had lots of rave reviews on google and was a “not miss” but for me it was a bit overhyped. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2340,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1045.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-2340" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the garden (which was just outside the Medina gates) we walked across the street to the spa (also known as a hammam) that we booked the day before. hammams are very typical in Morocco basically every neighborhood has or had one. Usually they’re public and divided by gender but the one we went to was a bit fancier. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Becca and I ended up getting a traditional black sand scrub in a steam room and then getting a massage. The scrub was a bit awkward because they kept opening up the door to get in and out and the door led to the main hall which made us very visible to passerby’s. That said the scrub felt really good and it was kinda gross how much dead skin came off! The massage was also good, not as strong as I was hoping but they never are. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the spa we walked back to the riad (it was about 1.4 miles away). At the riad I took my covid test which was negative (yay!). Becca and I had booked a food tour that met at 6 that in the main square so we walked there and met our tour guide, Abdul, and other member in our group who was a lady from Iceland (she was visiting her niece who married a Moroccan man and has 4 kids here!). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The food tour was really fun we had a bunch of stops. At the first stop we had tangia (different than tangine) and sheep head! The tangia was good! The sheep head was interesting…it all mostly just tasted chewy. We tried all the different parts of the head: the toungue, the cheek, and the eyeball! The eyeball was kinda gross because you had to dig it out with your finger and it was really slimy mushy, but it didn’t taste that bad though! We ate everything with just our hands, that’s the traditional Moroccan way, they don’t use silverware according to our guide. Also apparently they eat sheep head in Iceland because the lady we were with acted like it was no big deal and she really liked the tongue. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The second stop was an olive shop where we tried a bunch of different types of olives. I’m not a huge fan of olives but these tasted good. Our next stop was a soup and pancake shop. The soup was a tomato based soup with noodles that tasted really good. Then we got a pancake that was made with onions and spices that was also tasty. Unfortunately  Becca couldn’t eat the soup or pancake. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2343,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1054.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-2343" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our next stop was a small little shop that had little meat patties and bread to make into a small sandwich. Our guide asked us to guess what the meat was, we had no idea we guessed basically every animal and it was all wrong. It turned out it was anchovies, never would’ve guessed it, but they were surprisingly good! </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Next stop was a doughnut shop they make the dough 12 hours in advance and fry it and put honey on it. They were pretty good!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After that we went through a back alley to a “restaurant” that made corn based cuscous, the best in Marrakech according to our guide. It was really good! The restaurant has been run by the same lady for years, there are no signs, it’s not on a map or google, there’s no menu, you have to call in your order ahead of time and she’ll go get the ingredients in the morning and cook. She’s known as the mother of the Medina according to our guide. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the couscous we continued walking through the Messina and made a stop in a random door to see where they heat up the public hammams. They keep the fire going all day to heat the water and steam and they also will cook the taginas here! Our guide said that this guy will burn anything for you if he likes you haha. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2344,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1060.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-2344" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Continuing on we stopped in another small door and saw where they make a lot of the bread for the Medina. They have a huge oven and take dough from all the shops and cook it. They can tell who the bread is for based on the cloth and tray that the dough comes in. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2345,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1061.jpg?w=1024" alt="" class="wp-image-2345" /><figcaption>The oven is huge and can hold over 300 of those bread circles</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our next food stop was a small snail stand in the market. Snails are a typical cheap snack for Moroccans, we had seen them in some other cities too. You pull the snail out with a toothpick to eat it or you can suck it out. They were pretty slimy and salty but they weren’t too bad! Kind of reminded me of mussels but softer. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2346,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1066.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-2346" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our final stop was a pizza store. We didn’t have pizza here though just smoothies and cookies (some that Becca could eat). I had the mixed fruit smoothie with milk and Becca had the avocado smoothie which was surprisingly good. I don’t think they use ice for the smoothies so they were more like a juice consistency but still yummy. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2347,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1067.jpg?w=768" alt="" class="wp-image-2347" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tour ended in the main square. Our guide helped set us up a ride to the airport for tomorrow and then we made our way back through the Medina to our riad. Jamie and co returned shortly from dinner we hung out for a bit and got all packed and ready for our trip home tomorrow. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day 10</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up at 4:30, got ready and met our ride. They walked to meet us at our riad and then we walked with them to the car (parked a few blocks away since cars can’t fit in the streets by our riad). The aiport was ~15min away, getting through security to the gate was fairly easy. We spent the last of our Moroccan dirham on some snacks for the flight. Our first connection was Paris, we got there earlier enough to get on an earlier flight back to Atlanta. The ticket agent originally said Becca could switch for free but it was going to cost me $1,000 haha, obviously that was wrong and he eventually was able to switch us both to the earlier flight (arrived ~2.5 hours earlier in ATL) after about 30 minutes. The flight to Atlanta was also easy, it was a pretty empty flight so we had a ton of space. When we landed (just before 6) we were hoping to catch the earlier flight back to Savannah at 7:20p instead of 10:55p. Because I had global entry I was able to zip through passport control, switch my ticket to the earlier flight and make it through security again and all the way to the new gate in time....Becca however did not have global entry and it took her literally <strong>2.5 hours</strong> to get to through the passport control line and to meet me at the gate!! So sadly we missed the early flight so  we had an extra 3.5 hours at the airport. It was honestly lucky we got the early flight from Paris because if we didn't I'm not sure Becca would've made the 10:55 flight (last flight to Savannah for the day). We ended up making it back home around 12:30am, unpacked a little and then went straight to bed.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Overall it was a really fun trip! I highly recommend checking out Morocco, it's a really unique country filled with a huge variety of landscapes with tons of fun activities. As always  I'm super grateful to be able to take these fun trips with my family and friends. Time to start planning the next one!</p>
