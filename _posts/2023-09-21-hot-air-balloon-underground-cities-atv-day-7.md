<p>Today we woke up super early at 5:10 and got picked up from our hotel shortly after 5:30 for hot air ballooning! Cappadocia is known for its hot air balloons, so this was a must do for us. We were the last ones picked up in our transport van and we made our way ~15 minutes directly to the balloon launch site. The balloon company was called THK and recommended by our host. Every morning over one hundred balloons fly, and some of the companies are more sketchy than others so it was good to go with a trusted one.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There were a few other balloons at our launch site setting up. It took them about 20 minutes to get the balloon rigged up and inflated. There were already a bunch of balloons in the air so we took some pictures of them while we waited.</p>

<p>The flight lasted about an hour. It was really cool to see all the balloons up in the air- I lost count around 100. The balloons don’t have any real control besides altitude, so they end up drifting whichever way the wind blows them. Once in the air we saw a very ominous cloud of dark smoke - we later found out that one of the buildings in the town caught on fire (at first we thought maybe it was a balloon crash).</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ended up drifting over the town of Goreme and then landed in a nearby field. The pilot and crew did an excellent job landing, putting it down directly on the trailer. After they strapped down the basket we unloaded and they had a celebratory non-alcoholic champagne and flight certificates for us lol.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/LzdPFIV.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the celebration we were dropped back off at our hotel. We took a nap before getting breakfast on the terrace at 8:30. Breakfast was a buffet with tons of options, they even had gluten free bread for Becca.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stayed at the Sora Cave Hotel. It has an upper terrace, and the best view is below. Goreme is pretty hilly, and this hotel is near the top. It is about a ten minute walk up the road to an excellent balloon view point (we went up another morning). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/EmpaS1l.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Breakfast buffet on the terrace</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we rested in our room for another hour before heading out to the underground city of Derikuynu. Derikuynu is about 30 minutes south by car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The parking guard tried to get us to pay for parking on the street next to the entrance, we ended up driving literally a block over and parking for free (kind of a scam, there was tons of parking). Entrance price was about $8 a person for the cave. This cave is apparently the deepest in the area and was thought to hold 100,000-300,000 people.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The cave was cool, luckily not too crowded. There was a worker at the main cave entrance who gave us a tour of the first level before asking if we wanted an official tour, which he tried to sell us for $30 so we politely declined. We explored the rest of the cave system. There were tons of tunnels and different rooms. They had an area for all the horses and live stock, wells, churches, wineries, basically everything you would need to live. When the community out grew their current living situation, they would just carve out more rooms. Some of the tunnels got really tight- I explored them while Becca waited. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We explored around the the underground city for about an hour, it was actually smaller than I was expecting it to be. They don’t allow tourists to access the entire system. Once we got out we took a quick picture with the city sign before getting back in the car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/O3sBkbS.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our next stop was back towards Goreme at a town called Kaymakli that had another underground city. Although this cave system was supposedly smaller it definitely felt a lot more expansive- higher ceilings and better added lighting and infrastructure. Because of that there were a TON of tour groups and people. Which made it very slow to move around in since many of the passageways only fit one person. The other problem is the cave system doesn’t have the best ventilation so it was pretty hot and humid which results in lots of crowded people sweating and not the best smelling cave haha.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/Q6aDNAe.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We explored around for probably 40 minutes before making our way back up to the surface. The entrance area had a pretty cool model of the cave system. There is a theory (unproved) that all the different caves in the area and ones 100s of miles away are all connected by underground tunnels.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/X0fqS0x.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">3D model of the Kaymakli cave</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There was a large empty parking lot area near where we parked our car, so we took advantage of the opportunity and I showed Becca how to drive stick shift. She did super well! After practicing around the parking lot for 20 minutes we switched back and I drove to Goreme and we stopped at an Indian restaurant for lunch.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We both had the chicken tikka Masala with rice and I also got garlic naan. Very tasty! The price of the food was pretty expensive but at least it was good!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/l20bC0P.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After lunch we made a quick stop in town at Oze coffee shop, it’s a coffee shop owned by our hotel so our first drink was free. We tried a Turkish coffee - basically a very strong dark coffee. It wasn’t too bad just a little gritty for me.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/7orvw6O.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Turkish coffee</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Afterwards we made our way back to our hotel to rest until around 4:30 pm when we were picked up for our ATV tour. Originally Becca wasn’t going to go originally, but changed her mind when she found out it was on set dirt roads and not free roaming. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ATV tour started at the main office area which had to have over 100 ATVs- there were so many of them! We paid, got helmets and our ATV. I drove the first leg which was to the sword valley ~15 minutes away. The tour was as I expected, follow the leader not going very fast or doing anything wild on the ATVs, but the scenery was nice!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/NRWqR0J.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stopped at sword valley for ~10 minutes, we took some pictures and I droned before getting back on the ATVs. There were tons of ATV groups, I think our group had about 12 ATVs (24 people) and 2 guides.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After sword valley Becca drove the ATV to our next stop (she actually drove more aggressively than me!). Our next stop was the lookout point of love valley. The annoying bit about the ATV tour was there was sooo much dust when driving, we did our best to drive to the side of people (instead of directly behind them) to avoid the dust cloud but it was really unavoidable.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the love valley viewpoint we took some pictures and I flew the drone some more before we got back on the ATVs and made our way to our final stop at sunset point.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Luckily we got to the sunset point relatively early so there weren’t as many people there. We got some nice pictures and drone shots on the point before a TON of people showed up. Disliking the massive crowd Becca and I hiked ~5min over to another spot that was much less crowded and also had some awesome views of the valley and the sunset.</p>

<p>After sunset we got back on the ATVs and made our way back to the starting office point. After dropping off the ATVs we got dropped back off at our hotel. We decided to hang out on the terrace and relax for a bit, I had got some Turkish wine to try out and also was researching potential routes for mountain biking tomorrow.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After hanging out for about an hour we were hungry so we walked down to the main strip of the city and had dinner at a place called Fatboys. The food was really good, I had a meat stew thing and we split some nachos.</p>
