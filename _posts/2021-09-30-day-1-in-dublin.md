<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Travel from Savannah to Chicago and then onto Dublin with United went off without a hitch. My dad arrived before me around 5:15am and got the rental car sorted. I arrived at 9a, my golf bag was literally the first bag out (off to a good start!). I quickly made my way through customs and met my dad right outside. We stopped at the car rental kiosk to get my drivers license scanned (since I’ll be doing most of the driving) and then walked to the car and packed the rest of my luggage. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our car was a decent sized opel, we had to fold down the back seat to fit our clubs, but it was plenty big for the two of us. We got a manual (much cheaper than automatic), which I personally enjoy driving, but the other factor was the drivers side is reversed so not only are you driving on the opposite side but you’re also shifting with your opposite hand. That plus the lack of sleep and trying to navigate wasn’t a great combo. I definitely had a few close calls just getting out of the parking lot!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Nevertheless, after about a 20min drive (a little longer than it should be been since the roundabouts with the GPS were confusing and I missed an exit) we made it to our Airbnb just outside the city center. We were early so we decided to stop at a cafe down the block to get some hot chocolate, and I had to do a bit of work. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stayed at the cafe until about 11:15 and then made our way back to the Airbnb, luckily our host was there and was happy to let us in early to drop our bags and have some tea and cookies (even though check in technically wasn’t until 3). Our hosts name was Dermont and he was super friendly!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After some tea we decided to walk (~15min) to the Guinness factory. It was a bit confusing to find the entrance since there were a few buildings (the tour entrance is at the storehouse). The tour was self-guided and basically you started at the bottom level and made your way up ~7 levels or so until you go to the top which was a bar whee you could try the beer and have great views of the city (it’s the highest bar in Dublin!). Overall it was pretty cool. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I had to jump on a few work calls while we hung out at the top (not a bad place to work actually). And then we made our way toward our next stop St Patrick’s cathedral. On the way we stopped at a small restaurant called tasty 8. I had a Chinese bbq chicken wrap and my dad had a chicken salad, both were tasty! </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stopped shortly at st Patrick’s cathedral, it was a pretty standard old church, nothing too unique, but it was still cool to see. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Next we walked another 10min or so to the Dublin “castle”. It really just looked like a building except for one section of it that had a stone tower. The castle ended up having free weekday admission till the end of 2021 which is great because I definitely wouldn’t have been happy if I paid for it (normally it’s $20). Essentially it was an art gallery with a bunch of old paintings and portraits (not really my thing). There was some really cool sand castles that were built in the courtyard. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":2083,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/img_1999.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-2083" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the castle we walked to trinity college. This one was also $20 to enter, we weren’t really sure what all there was to see inside besides a really old book, so we decided to skip it and grab some gelato instead. We ate at a nearby park. After we finished we walked down a famous pedestrian street called grafton street. It was filled with a bunch of shops and street performers. We ended up watching an Irish tap dancer guy for a couple minutes, he was actually pretty impressive. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At this point our phones were dying at we still needed to book our hotels for the next few days so we walked back about 45 min to our Airbnb, on the way back we walked past an escape room so we decided to stop in and check it out. Unfortunately they didn’t have any times available now but the lady was super friendly and convinced us to book the 8:45 time.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we got back to the Airbnb (around 4:30) we rested for a bit while we booked our hotels and also a golf course for tomorrow. We chatted with our host and I did a bit more work. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Around 7:45 we booked an Uber to the escape room. Before going in we stopped at a nearby “takeaway” restaurant. I got a diner kebab, which is always delicious and cheap! We ended up walking back over to the escape room and eating in the lobby before our game started. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The escape room itself was awesome. The room we choose (they have 3) was called “Game of Stones”, which for legal purposes has absolutely nothing to do with the similarly named HBO show Game of thrones :). The essential point of this room was to search the royal quarters and find a dragons egg. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The puzzles were really unique, I won’t give anything away but there were a lot of really cool ones, and only a few lock/combos. Although I wouldn’t recommend doing an escape room while be exhausted from a day of travel and lack of sleep, we still managed to find the dragon egg and escape! We got out with about 10 min to spare and the game master only gave us 3 hints. She said normally people finish with less than 5 minutes and need about 6-8 hints. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
