<p style="text-align:justify;">Today we woke up early again (this time out of habit) and decided to walk around the trails by our hut. The land the hostel was on actually had its own ruins (pretty much everything in Belize is ruins if you dig deep enough). As we started to walk the trail one of the dogs from the other day must have saw us and sprinted up to us and started to lead us down the path (it was really cool). The trail was pretty short but had some pretty nice views. It was interesting to see the ruins there because they were in the process of excavating them. After about 30 minutes we started to head back and the dog showed us right back to our hut. We showered and then made our way to the dinning hut for breakfast. The breakfast was pretty basic (eggs, beans, and sausage).</p>

<p style="text-align:justify;">Afterwards we packed up our stuff and headed out in the car. Once we got back on the main road we drove about 15 minutes farther east to a grocery store called Benjis to meet our tour guide. After about 5 minutes our guide showed up in a van with 4 other people to take us into the jungle.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">The ride to the start of the trail took about 30 minutes and was one of the worst roads I've ever seen. There were massive pot holes everywhere and we even had to drive through a small stream. By this point we were pretty deep into the jungle at a place called the tapir reserve. The vans let us off at a picnic area and we started our 2.5 mile hike to get to the cave entrance. The hike was really flat but we had to cross a river 3 times (not just a small stream we literally had to swim across holding a rope because it was too deep for me to touch). My shoes were not really meant for water so they were completely flooded which made hiking all the more uncomfortable. Unfortunately for us they didn't allow us to bring anything with us so we couldn't take any pictures along the way :(. I guess some tourists a few years ago dropped their cameras while taking pictures of one of the skeletons in the cave and damaged it. The views of the jungle on the hike were pretty incredible. The jungle itself it so dense and diverse.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After about a half hour of hiking we made it to the entrance of the cave. The cave itself was actually formed by a river so to enter the cave we actually had to swim in. At this point we were completely soaked anyway so it wasn't a big deal. We spent a good hour going into the cave twisting and squeezing to get past some of the tight areas. We eventually had to climb up the cave and once we did we were surrounded by Mayan artifacts (mostly broken pots). The Mayans believed that the caves were the entrances into hell (what they called xibalba). They would come into the caves to preform rituals and to make sacrifices (most of the time human) in hopes of pleasing the Gods. We also came across a few skeletons that had become preserved by the cave dust. It was really incredible to see. Once we got out of the cave Rachel and I hiked ahead of the group back to the picnic area (we were getting short on time and we needed to make it to the port before the last ferry left at 5:30). Unfortunately we had to wait for a van to leave and take us back our car. Also one of the older couple that were with us really wanted a ride to Belize, so of course we said that it would be fine, little did we know that these two were the super slow and it felt like an eternity before they got all their stuff packed up. Once the van got us to the car it was pedal to the metal back to Belize. I was passing people left and right! We made it to the car rental drop off place at 4:30 and then got a shuttle from there to the port and had 40 minutes to spare before the last boat left!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">We took the boat out to Ambergris Caye which was about an hour and a half away. Once on the island we got to our hostel which was less than 100 feet from the pier. We dropped our stuff off and grabbed some street tacos from one of the local vendors for dinner (all the restaurants were closed because it was a Sunday night).</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Tomorrow I head to the dive shop to start my 3 half days of SCUBA training!</p>
