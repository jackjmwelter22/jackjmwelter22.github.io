<p style="text-align:justify;">I slept great last night! I think I may have actually beat the jet leg by staying up so long. We woke up today around 7am and had the "American" breakfast at our hostel which consisted of eggs, ham, sausage, and toast.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After breakfast we took the subway south of the city center to the temple of heaven park. One thing you notice about Beijing is even though everything seems close it's all really really spread out, which makes it difficult to walk anywhere. At the temple of heaven park we saw...the temple of heaven! Shocker right? It was basically another big pagoda in the middle of the park. I asked a Chinese guy to take a picture of jamie and I. You'd think Chinese people would be good at taking pictures right? Wrong. After 3 attempts this was the best he got. Totally cut off half the pagoda!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-404" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0860.jpg" width="4032" height="3024"></p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">I guess it must be the Japanese that are good picture takers 😂. In order to avoid any other bad pictures I ended up buying a selfie stick, which I've put to good use even though it makes me look like an idiot. Anyway there were a couple more temples in the park and this echo spot called the mound that was pretty cool. It was in the middle of this circle where you could hear yourself echo if you talked (Purdue had something similar on campus).</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-406" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0871.jpg" width="3024" height="4032">Probably one of the tallest people in china</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After exploring the park we made our way back to the subway to head towards Tiananmen Square. In order to get into the square you had to go way out of the way to go to the security checkpoint which consisted of a long line of people. The square itself was pretty uneventful, just a large open area of concrete really. We also climbed up one of the old city gate entrances that was near the entrance to the square.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-405" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0875.jpg" width="2576" height="1932"></p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">weird thing we saw were kids with, presumably, potty training pants. These were basically crotchless pants for babies (I guess so there was easy access to the diaper? Idk it was strange but they all wore them). Also Beijing in general is very dirty, dusty &amp; smoggy. There is a visible haze over the city, I really don't blame the locals for wearing surgical masks.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">Another thing about the square is they don't make a big deal about the famous protestor who stood in front of the tanks. In fact the picture is blocked on the Chinese internet and I'm not really sure how many people are aware of it like they are in the US.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After the square we made our way to see the forbidden city. This is where the old emperors and their families used to live when they ruled over china. Again there were a ton of temples/pagodas to see and a really cool imperial garden.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-408" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0900-1.jpg" width="4032" height="3024"></p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After the forbidden city we stopped at the hostel and then set off to find lunch. We were looking for a restaurant called quanjude that was famous for roast duck (the best in Beijing I'm told). Our first attempt was to use google maps to find it. Well that completely failed, there wasn't a single restaurant near where it said quanjude should be. We talked to a tourist information hut and they pointed us down a busy street and gestured that the restaurant was on the left side. After walking around with no luck for 20 minutes we were on the verge of giving up. I was furiously googling to see if there were any hints as to where this place would be. I stumbled across some pictures of the restaurant store front. It was really inconspicuous with just some Chinese lettering and a door. Then I looked up from where we were sitting and there it was! Right in front of us 😂. It turns out you had to go inside this nondescript building and take the elevator down to the basement...super obvious.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-409" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0907.jpg" width="4032" height="3024"> Once we were there the restaurant ended up being really cool! And the roasted duck was delicious! What you do is take this pancake/tortilla and make what I'd call a duck taco with the roast duck and some random veggies. Yumm!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After eating we walked to see the Bell and drum towers which were nearby. These were big towers that they used like clock towers to bang drums and bells every quarter hour. We climbed up both towers (the steps were super steep!) and got to see a demonstration of how they would play drums.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-410" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0912.jpg" width="3024" height="4032"> The hike up the tower</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After the towers we decided to go to the central perk cafe. It's basically a replica of the coffee shop from Friends and since I'm a huge fan of the show I though it would be fun. So we fired up the google maps and off we went. It wasn't very close so the subway ride and everything took about 40 minutes along with about a mile of walking. Well....when we get to where it's supposed to be it was nowhere to be seen! Dammit google! Failed us again! After doing some research I found out that the cafe is actually on the complete opposite side of town! Super convenient... 😫. At this point we had already walked about 13 miles and Jamie was clearly pissed off I convinced her to go this far 😂 to see a cafe that wasn't even there.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">We ended up abandoning the cafe (maybe next time) and made our way back by the hostel to a bar called Great leap brewing. I made sure to double check the place was actually going to be there before we left this time. It took another 40 minutes and more walking to get there but it was worth it! It seems like this was the only place to get craft beer in all of Beijing (I guess it's not really a thing here). The atmosphere of the place was also really cool, even though it was in the middle of the city it had its own secluded little courtyard.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-411" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0917.jpg" width="4032" height="3024"> Another note about Beijing is there are a TON of Asians. Now I know that might seem obvious but I was also expecting there to be a lot of tourists at all these different sites of different ethnicities. Nope, in fact the most white people we saw in one place since we arrived were at the brewery/bar!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">After drinking a few good beers we decided to make our way to the Wangfujing night market. This market is know for its 'exotic' foods and if you know anything about me I love trying crazy foods! To get to the market we decided to try taking the bus, according to google it was the fastest way to get there. It actually ended up being way easier than I thought! We waited at the stop and hopped on bus #82 for 2 yuan (like 30cents) and were dropped off at the market in 15 minutes.</p>
<p style="text-align:left;"><img class="wp-image-412" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0918.jpg" width="1932" height="2576">definitely stood out on the bus<img class="wp-image-413" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0928.jpg" width="3024" height="4032"> The night market was really cool! Tons of shops selling all kinds of snacks. In no time I found what I was looking for...fried scorpions! 🦂🦂🦂</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;">They looked super disgusting! They even had live ones that were still moving skewered and waiting to be fried.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"> I was feeling confident after the two IPAs I had earlier, but Before I decided to try one I bought another beer from the next stand over so that I had something to wash it down with and avoid puking. It turns out they conveniently only sell them 3 to a stick so even though I only wanted 1 I ended up with 3 of these things! I decided to say "fuck it" and closed my eyes and bit one off the skewer!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-415" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0934.jpg" width="1959" height="1957"> Honestly, it didn't taste half bad! It was really crunchy and tasted crispy. I was trying to see if anyone wanted to try one since I had two more left but nobody was daring enough so I decided I might as well eat another one 😂. After eating two I successfully convinced Jamie that they weren't bad and she ate the last one!</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-416" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/output.jpg" width="1960" height="2616"> We ended the night by walking around the rest of the market and getting a bubble waffle with ice cream to get rid of any after taste from the scorpions.</p>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><img class="wp-image-417" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/img_0931.jpg" width="3024" height="4032"> We made our way back to the hostel by subway. It felt amazing to finally take my shoes off and lay down! We walked a total of 16.5 miles! My feet were sore and actually had a few blisters of my toes 😳. Tomorrow is going to be another long day of walking. We're heading out on a tour to one of the more remote sections of the Great Wall to do some hiking.</p>
