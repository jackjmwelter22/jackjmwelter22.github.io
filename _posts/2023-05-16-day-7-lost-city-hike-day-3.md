<p>Since we were stopping back at this camp for lunch we were able to leave some of our things here and have a lighter pack for the climb. My pack was still pretty heavy since I was carrying the drone but it was nice not to have to carry all of my clothes (I was able to leave some out to dry while we hiked). One thing I forgot to mention is that it is hot here! Like seriously hot. I've been hiking at a pretty quick pace which hasn't helped which leaves me drenched after hiking - so getting the clothes to dry is great. A few people in our group didn't bring fresh clothes for each day and I seriously don't know how they do it.</p>

I flew my drone around a bit to get some cool pictures while I waited.</p>

<p>Once the rest of the group arrived our guide told us a little bit about the lost city. At first I thought that what we were seeing was it, which if I was honest was a little underwhelming after all the hiking and effort it took to get here. I was excited to find out that there were hundreds of these circles and that this was only the first section of the lost city. Some other interesting bits of information, all the circles used to be houses (there is another area where some of the tribe now lives), the city was built over 1,000 years ago and was "lost" - when it was abandoned by the Tairona people during the Spanish colonization - until it was discovered in the 1970s by treasure hunters. Since then the government took steps to protect the city.</p>

<p>Since our group was the last to leave in the morning we had the benefit of being the last ones in the lost city which meant there was no one else but us when we left each section. The lost city is often described as the Machu Picchu of Colombia - I think Machu Picchu is just as "grand" if not more so however the difference is there are way more people at Machu Picchu (since they are literally buses up there). The isolation and solitude of the lost city was super cool and in my opinion makes it better than Machu Picchu.</p>

<p>After we finished the snack plate. which was delicious. We started to make our way to another part of the city when we ran into the Mamo (basically the leader of the tribe). Apparently our guide knows him and I guess he's kind of a big deal since he's on the 50,000 peso bill.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We bought bracelets from the Mamo which support the tribe (they were donation based but it's suggested 2 pesos which is like 50 cents USD). Our guide is in the process of helping film a documentary so we also saw the Mamo sign some sort of permission agreement for the film.</p>

<p>After relaxing in the river I hung clothes out to dry and had some hot chocolate. We played some more of the Tuepen card game and ate popcorn before having dinner. We also learned a sad story about our translator - apparently she was in a car wreck with her family and was the only one to survive - she was in a coma and had broke both her legs. She loves hiking the trail because at one point she thought she'd never be able to walk again, crazy. </p>
