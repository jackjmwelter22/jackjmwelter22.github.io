<p>Today was an early day.  We woke up at 4:15am to get a taxi to the airport to catch our 6:30am flight. We got to the airport and through security without issue and had a small breakfast of some pastries and hot chocolate before boarding. Very delish!</p>

<p> The flight to Medellin was a super short hop, a bit over 100 nautical miles. However, driving through the mountains would have taken over 8 hours! The 30 minute flight was smooth. We arrived in Medellin a little after 7. Unfortunately we weren’t able to connect with our scheduled ride, but we were able to quickly get a taxi which actually ended up being a few dollars cheaper. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We arrived at our hostel, Los Patios, in La poblana barrio which is a very trendy area popular for tourists. We checked in and dropped off our bags (our room wasn’t ready). We explored the hostel and the surrounding area. After the quick walk we hung out in the hostel lobby until our 10am fruit tour. </p>

<p>The fruit tour was us, our guide Marcela, and one other person, Rodrigo, who was also from the US but originally from Mexico. We drove about 20 minutes to the plaza Minorista market. Along the way we learned a little about our guide. She was leaving after our tour to go live in New York with her husband who she “spiritually married” (no idea what that means). Needless to say a little strange, but otherwise very nice!</p>

<p>Our first stop in plaza Minorista was a small restaurant that was one of the first in the plaza. There we had the traditional drink calledAgua de panella also called guandolo- a sugarcane drink infused with passion fruit. </p>

<p>While drinking our agua de panella our guide showed us flash cards of all the fruits we would be looking for and trying today. It was a really fun tour we ended up roaming around the market searching for the foods on the cards and learning about them. Our guide kept telling us about how clean the market was, but to me it looked kind of dirty 😁. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we met our tour group at the subway station to begin our tour of comuna 13. There is so much history behind comuna 13- they made a few documentaries about it. Basically it was a really bad neighborhood because it was the access point between Medellin and the coast and jungle (where all the drugs were being farmed). Therefore whoever controlled the comuna controlled all trade and drug traffic - this made it a highly contested area between the guerrillas (various groups of political soldiers), paramilitary (private military), and the government. The government left the area mostly alone until Pablo Escobar was gone and they finally had the resources to try and “clean up” the city. These government operations ended up killing a lot of innocent people, specifically the Orión mission. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In order to make amends the government invested in a lot of social projects like schools for the comuna.  However, the one that proved to be the best for the comuna was the escalators (the comuna is on a mountain). The escaltors facilitated the movement but also had the unintended consequence of bringing lots of tourists to the area which was a major boon for the local economy. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our tour guide Christian was awesome. He took us all throughout the comuna showing us different types of graffiti and street art and the maze of alleys in the unplanned city. He is from the comuna and is also a teacher. He told us a crazy story about how his dad was shot in the face 3 times (being accused of being a guerrilla) and survived!</p>
