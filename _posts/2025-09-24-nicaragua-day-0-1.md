In Miami our connection gate was in the same terminal, and we had plenty of time to get there - much better than our recent experiences sprinting through the Miami airport! The flight from Miami to Managua was also easy, just over two hours and Bennett slept for over half of it!</p>

<p>Everything was going so smoothly, it was refreshing! That is until we went through customs in Nicaragua and my bag got flagged… it turns out Nicaragua has a very strict no drones policy. I was starting to freak out because I didn’t want to lose my drone- especially to customs. There was another person there who had a drone that was also being confiscated. The customs agents ended up taking lots of pictures and letting me take a picture of the "form" they filled out. We also had some tape with us so we ended up quickly labeling everything with my name and information. Once it was all documented they put my drone in an old liquor box...hopefully I see it again!</p>

<p>After leaving customs we got our rental car. The car rental was smooth- a quick shuttle across the street and no surprises. We had a small manual Suzuki Dzire. From the airport we had a 2 hour drive to the city of Leon. Bennett slept the entire car ride - he's such a trooper! We’re staying at the Hotel el Convento- one of the best in the city but still inexpensive. After checking in and unpacking, we explored the hotel and the pool.</p>

<p>We got ready and Becca walked with me to the Bigfoot Hostel where I got my refund for the previously cancelled tour. The other tour was directly across the street in another hostel called Via Via. The tour was done through Volcano Day. I did the basic tour for $25. The only difference between the basic and “all-in” tour was you paid $10 more for a shirt and sandwich.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I said bye to Becca and Bennett as my tour left for the volcano around 9am. We took a big truck that drove us ~1 hour to the entrance of the nature reserve, and then to the parking lot at the start of the hike. The horn on the truck was obnoxiously loud, like it seriously hurt your ears and the driver was happy to honk it every chance he got!</p>

I think we took more breaks than actual hiking (there were a few very slow people that were very out of shape and clearly not hikers, they even paid extra for someone to carry their board up). At the breaks we took some pictures the view of the volcano and surrounding area was beautiful.</p>

<p>It took around an hour to get to the top (including the stops). At the top we left our boards and walked along the ridge of the volcano to see the crater. The ground is actually super hot when you dig down an inch past the top layer - you can actually like a cigarette or cook and egg with the ground.</p>

You are not able to wear your backpack on your back, it has to go in the front,  which looks a bit silly. The basic instructions were to lean back and keep your feet off the board (for stability).</p>

<p>I ended up fishtailing and wiping out near the end of the run. Overall the run took about 2 minutes to sled down. It was a fun and unique experience. I do wish I could’ve done it multiple times since the hike really was pretty short. I could’ve definitely done it at least twice in the time it took the slow people to hike up.</p>
