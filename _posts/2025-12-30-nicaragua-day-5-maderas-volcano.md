<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up at 5 AM, thanks again Bennett :-). We hung out in the room until 6:30 and then made our way up to reception for breakfast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because I was doing the volcano hike they had an early breakfast already prepared for me at 6:45. I had the scrambled eggs and toast.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating, I met my guide Moises, got my sandwich from the kitchen for lunch on the trail, my $3 entrance ticket, and then we loaded up into our car.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I think technically you don’t need a guide to hike the volcano. However, everything I read online recommended it because the trail is confusing. It was $45 for a private guide which is a bit steep for Nicaragua, but I wanted to do the hike. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We drove about 10 minutes from the lodge to get to the entrance of the trail. The entire trail was in the forest and was very steep. Luckily it hadn’t rained much, but you could tell if it was wet it would be very, very muddy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After about an hour of hiking, we made it to the Mirador, which is a viewpoint where you can see the other volcano. Unfortunately, it was cloudy so all we could see was some of the coastline.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://i.imgur.com/LjqExtd.jpeg","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"embed-handler","responsive":true} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We continued hiking. I was actually leading the hike and it seemed pretty easy to follow the trail. I’m not sure I really needed a guide at all. The guide was super friendly though. We talked mostly in Spanish so I could practice, but his English was pretty good. We talked about sports, work, life, politics, just about everything lol.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:embed {"url":"https://i.imgur.com/f5dLbmj.jpeg","type":"rich","providerNameSlug":"embed-handler","responsive":true} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was super hot and I was drenched in sweat, my shoes were actually starting to get soggy. It was pretty gross! The last bit of the trail was also fairly muddy, even though there wasn’t a whole lot of rain. We made it to the peak after two hours. Luckily the clouds had cleared and we were able to get some really good views of the crater and the lagoon inside of it.</p>

<p>We hopped down into the crater, which took about 10 minutes, and then ate lunch. I had a snack bar and sandwich. The sandwich was skimpy on the meat. Because it hadn’t rained a lot the water level in the lagoon was too low to swim. I tried to walk to the edge to put my feet in and sank up to my shins in mud, not worth it!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating, relaxing, and taking some pictures, we started to make our way back down. The hike down was a bit quicker. We passed by several groups of people on their way up. We took one short break at the Mirador. The clouds had dispersed and we could see the volcano, so I took a few pictures.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From there we continued all the way back down to the car. The total time of the hike was about four hours (including the breaks and lunch). Originally the hotel receptionist told me it would take eight hours lol.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got back in the car and drove the 10 minutes back to the Toto Eco Lodge. I had a celebratory beer with the guide (his request lol). The national beer Toña was actually pretty good.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the beer and settling up I went back to our cabin to see Becca and Bennett. Bennet had just gone down for a nap so we hung out on our porch and relaxed in the hammock. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>Bennett woke up at 1:45 pm and we decided to go check out the “best beach in Ometepe”, Playa Santo Domingo. We made a quick stop at the Ocean Mart our or way and got some cash, drinks, and snacks. We ended up deciding to stop at a restaurant on the beach called Comedor Julia.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The restaurant was pretty janky, but was well reviewed on Google. The first table we sat down to had ants that started biting me, so we shifted a couple tables over. We ended up getting a fish to share. It was tilapia and it was really delicious.</p>

I had the chicken again and it was really good.</p>
