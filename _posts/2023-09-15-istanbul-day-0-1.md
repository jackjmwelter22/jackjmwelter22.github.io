<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I worked from home today, then we made our way to the airport to catch our 4:30pm flight to Philadelphia. We had a 4 hour layover in Philly, so we ate dinner at a smashburger place by our gate. Our second flight left at 11pm overnight to London. We arrived at Heathrow around 11am. Our final flight to Istanbul got delayed ~1 hour so we left at 2pm and landed around 8pm. We caught a taxi from the airport to our hotel in the center of the old city downtown ~1 hour drive. We checked in, our room was nice and so was the hotel. The spa was open until 10pm so I went down to try out the sauna for ~15min before it closed. After the sauna I went down the block to grab some late night food. I got a kebab and pide ( Turkish pizza) from a restaurant the hotel desk recommended. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Day 1</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up around 7:15 and ate breakfast downstairs at the hotel. The breakfast was a buffet and overall pretty good. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating we went back upstairs to get ready and then walked to the blue mosque, which was about half a mile away. it took a minute to find the entrance- there was only one, but there were a bunch of exits. Once we got in we were able to get into the blue mosque really quickly and take some pictures. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/jmLYqJb.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It looks really cool on the outside, to go inside you had to remove shoes, cover your knees and your shoulders, and women had to cover their heads. Surprisingly the blue mosque wasn’t really that blue inside. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/VTAzPvO.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the blue mosque, we walked literally across the street to Hagia Sophia, which is another large mosque. We realized the line for Hagia Sophia was way longer, and ended up having to walk almost all the way back to the blue mosque to get in line. The line went relatively quickly. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/F0OQWmh.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Line for hagia Sophia </figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It only took about 15 minutes to get inside, this mosque was much bigger and older looking than the blue mosque. We stayed inside for 5 to 10 minutes before heading out. Both Hagia Sophia and the blue mosque had free admission, but you have to time your visit around the prayer times. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/miAJxfo.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Inside Hagia Sophia</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/xuCAR2R.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Side of the Hagia Sophia </figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Next we went a little further up to the main gate of the Topkapi palace. The palace is a huge complex. You had to pay $35 each to get in (which to be honest was a bit much, however the other two were free, so it balanced out). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We started by going to the harem of the palace. It was a secluded corner with lots of rooms where the 500 concubines lived. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/CyxUAop.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Secluded halls of the harem</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the harem we made our way counterclockwise around the grounds of the palace. There were tons of different areas and rooms you could go in- including the throne, hammams, the kitchen, etc. In one of the areas they had a museum of relics, which consisted of a bunch of random items the most interesting been the prophet Muhammad’s tooth and lots of his beard hair, they are very proud of these items.They also had lots of gemstones and a famous Topkapi dagger. After exploring with felt like over 100 rooms, we decided to leave the palace.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/dspjJbX.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">One of the many rooms in the palace</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/5pOsVcg.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Terrace of the palace</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>On our way out we stopped at an ice cream shop where they did a little show before giving you the ice cream. It was pretty funny and the ice cream was pretty tasty (I had chocolate and pistachio). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/w7a2FuU.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the ice cream we continued on to Gülhane Park. This is a small park underneath the walls of the castle. We walked about a half a mile and saw the famous column of the goths, which is apparently one of the oldest Roman columns that still exists today. Honestly it wasn’t very impressive, but worth a quick stop since it’s right there. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/JhNvUVA.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The park let out right along the coast, so we walked down it. Here you could see both Europe and Asia being split by the Bosphorous strait. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/wNLzP2e.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Europe on the left Asia on the right</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We walked along the coast until we made it back to our hotel, which was again about half a mile away. We ate lunch at a small restaurant right by our hotel. I had a kebab wrap and Becca had kebab meat. It was really fast and good. We also tried Buffalo yoghurt, which was meh. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/tsYDuz3.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After eating, Becca was tired, so she decided to go back and rest at the hotel while I walked to the  Suleymaniye Mosque, which was about half a mile the other direction from our hotel. Along the way, I stopped at the Egyptian bazaar which was a big market with tons of spices and fruits and other food.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/cJHcRnI.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The mosque was on a hill and had a really cool views of the city and water. I went inside the mosque,  and it happened to be during prayer time which was interesting to see. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/y5P8O4s.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After visiting the mosque, I made my way back to the hotel along a different route to see the grand bazaar. This was essentially a huge street market with tons of different sections, grouped by the type of thing they were selling. For example, they had a gold section and a jewelry section. After walking through the bazaar I walked back to the hotel. Along the way I saw another ice cream shop so I had to try.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Back at the hotel, Becca and I went down to the spa to relax. I did the sauna and then we did the massage pool and the Turkish baths.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/rgof9sR.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the spa, we went up to the roof of the hotel to grab a snack before our Bosphorus cruise. We had hummus, and I tried the local beer. Views from the hotel were really nice. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/2pYIdqB.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We stopped back at the room after eating and I flew the drone a little bit to get some cool pictures.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/LikRZpC.jpg" alt="" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption">Hagia Sophia (left) Blue Mosque (right)</figcaption></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got picked up for our sunset cruise at 5:30. It was about a 10 minute drive to the port. The boat was fairly big and there were a ton of people. We got there fairly early so we were able to get a good seat. The worst part was some of the people felt like they had to take pictures of every single thing so they were constantly moving around and getting in our space to “get the shot”. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/ULlbTLz.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The cruise went up the Bosphorous strait about an hour, with a tour guide, explaining a bunch of the different buildings and monuments and attractions along the way. There were a ton of different mosques. Apparently the more minerets the more powerful the mosque, the most we saw was a newer mosque that had 6 minerets and was big enough for 40,000 people to pray at once (surprisingly only the eighth biggest in the world). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/3HoINth.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The tour included cookies and tea on the boat. The boat stopped on the Asia side for about an hour for shopping. However, there wasn’t really much to do on that side, so Becca and I walked down the street and then went back on the boat.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/a7SipCg.jpg" alt="" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the stop, the boat made its way back to the port, and we got dropped off back by our hotel. We made a quick stop for some dinner and Turkish delights- which we got to go and ate in our room before going to bed. </p>
