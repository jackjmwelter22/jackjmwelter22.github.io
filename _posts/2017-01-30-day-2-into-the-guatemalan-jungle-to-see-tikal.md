<p style="text-align:justify;"><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">Today was another early day. We woke up around 6am, grabbed a quick snack for breakfast in our room (cereal) and walked down to the tour office (about 2 min walk). There we met our tour guide Huge aka "juice" (not sure why he's called that). Luckily it was just Rachel and I on the tour so essentially we had our own private tour guide. We got in the tour van and headed towards the Guatemalan boarder on our way to see the famous Mayan ruins called Tikal. Getting through the boarder was painless and took like 5 minutes. Once we got through passport control we got into a new van with a Guatemalan driver (and our tour guide). The drive to Tikal took almost 2 hours. Along the way our tour guide gave us some background on the history of the Mayans which was really interesting. Once we arrived at the entrance of the park we ditched the van and trekked on foot deep into the Guatemalan jungle to get to the first small temples at Tikal. </span></span></p>

<div class="separator" style="clear:both;text-align:justify;"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em;" href="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/img_0684.jpg"><img class=" aligncenter" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/img_0684.jpg?w=225" width="300" height="400" border="0" /></a></div>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">
</span></span><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">What's really cool about Tikal is that they basically give you free reign to hike up the temples and go wherever you want. We really enjoyed hiking up and down the ruins and hearing the stories oh the ancient Mayan rituals that were performed (some of which are still practiced). I tried to volunteer Rachel to be sacrificed but apparently the don't do human sacrifices anymore :P</span></span>.</p>

<div class="separator" style="clear:both;text-align:justify;"></div>
<p style="text-align:justify;"><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">After the first small temples we stopped at some larger temples where king chocolate and his queen were buried. The whole site was HUGE, we saw like 10 temple/pyramid things (4 of which were ginormous). Apparently it took archaeologists 13 years to finish uncovering and restoring just two of them (they were essentially buried over time by the jungle growth). At the tops of the temples there were some amazing views of Tikal and the vast Guatemalan jungle. Walking around we also got to see some really cool plants and monkeys swinging around the trees. After walking around and hiking up temples for about 3 hours we were pretty exhausted made our way out of the park. On the drive back we stopped to get some lunch at a restaurant nearby. We were pretty hungry at this point! </span></span></p>

</span></span><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">After eating we started the long drive back to the boarder and then back to San Ignacio. Once we got back to San Ignacio we stopped to eat dinner at a restaurant called the Guava Limb cafe. The food was really good, we had a Ceviche appetizer and I had the Caribbean shrimp pasta. After eating we got back to our car and drove about 20 minutes East from San Ignacio to our next hostel which was essentially a jungle hut.</span></span></p>

<div class="separator" style="clear:both;text-align:justify;"><a style="margin-left:1em;margin-right:1em;" href="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/img_0719.jpg"><img class=" aligncenter" src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/img_0719.jpg?w=300" width="400" height="300" border="0" /></a></div>

</span></span><span style="background-color:white;"><span style="font-size:12.8px;">We arrived at the hostel (called Lower Dover) just after dark and we had to take a dirt road about 15 minutes to find it. Once we arrived we were greeted by 6 or so dogs and our host Mandy. She showed us to our hut and were the showers were (they were run off of rain water and heated by the sun. Mandy was actually from California originally and had bought the land (100 acres) when she was 30 and has been building on it ever since. We were pretty much beat at this point so we went to bed pretty early again. Tomorrow we head into the Belize jungle to do some spelunking!</span></span>
