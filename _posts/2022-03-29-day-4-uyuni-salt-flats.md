<p>The overnight bus was pretty nice, not the best sleep but at least 10x better than an airplane. We arrived in Uyuni around 7 am and met a guide who took us to a nearby breakfast restaurant called nonis. I had the americano breakfast it was pretty basic, toast, eggs, and ham. After breakfast we went tot the tour office to pay for the tour and start the tour. We ended up paying in both USD and Bolivianos since we were able to pay for the travel visa with credit card. However they rejected some of the USD because they were imperfect (small tears, etc) </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Apparently the tour wasn’t going to start for another two hours so we walked around the city to pick up some water. The city is super small and I think only exists because of the salt flats. The tour wasn’t very good at communicating not only was there a bit of a delay to the start of our tour but also we were told we needed to consolidate our luggage because “it would be too heavy”? So Becca and I combined into her pack, which is massive. Honestly the few clothes we took out wasn’t even close to the amount of weight we added in water. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Anyway, we finally start the tour 30 minutes later than we were told and the first stop was the Uyuni train graveyard. Basically a big area where all the old trains were left to rust away. Because of this you’re able to climb all over the trains and take a bunch of fun pictures. We hung out there for about 45 minutes and had a good time taking lots of pictures. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the train graveyard we made a quick stop to try on boots (I needed the largest pair they had available of course). There were a bunch of cute puppets hanging out while we tried on the boots. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After getting the boots we loaded up the car and made our way to colcahni, the town is the entrance to the salt flats and had a bunch of shops and things. Becca and I ended up getting an ornament made of salt that was in the shape of the salt flats. Also basically everything here is made of salt the buildings, chairs, etc all salt. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After 30 minutes we made our way back into the car and finally drove out onto the salt flats!! This was seriously one of the coolest places I’ve seen. The salt flats are soooo huge, it’s literally a dried up lake. It almost looks like you’re in a tundra of snow but instead of snow it’s all salt…all of it. Our first stop was a little water hole in the salt that was bubbling up, looking at it, you’d think it was hot but it was actually really cold. We sat around it and soaked our feet in the cold salt water for ~15min. Apparently it’s good for you. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>Next we drove to the Dakar monument…we’ll almost there. Our guides stopped short and had us walk about 15 minutes so they could drive ahead and set up lunch, kinda weird but whatever, hiking in the salty desert was kinda cool. The Dakar monument is a big monument I think for some race they do on the salt flats (our guide was more of a driver and didn’t tell us much). It was a cool looking monument though! There were also a bunch of flags in the ground, but sadly no American flag. After taking a few pictures we had a picnic lunch with everyone in our group (two cars with 6 people each). </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the sun went down we made our way back to colchani to get to our salt hotel. On the way the guide stopped and let us take some pictures of the stars. The salt hotel is pretty cool, again literally everything is made of salt from the floor to the walls, beds, etc. Becca and I took a quick shower and then went to get dinner with everyone. Dinner was much better than Lunch, it was salchipapas basically cut up hotdogs with fries and some other sauce and toppings. After dinner we went outside to stargaze some more then made our way to bed after a long exhausting day. </p>
