<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We went tot the airport after work to catch a quick flight over to Atlanta and then onto Munich. of course it wasn’t as smooth as we hoped (flying never seems to be now). Our plane was delayed about 45 min, getting in late from wherever it was coming from. We boarded quickly and taxi out to the runway when our pilot said we needed to wait for the weather to blow through. Well that took at least 30 min so now our super cushy 2 hour connection became a “sprint through the airport as fast as you can and hope you make it before the doors close” connection. We ended up landing around 5:20 and our connection was supposed to leave at 5:50 (with doors usually closing 15 min before that). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Delta had already anticipated we weren’t going to make it and pre-switched us to another (worse) flight with a connection. We sprinted through the airport from A terminal all the way to the international terminal. I arrived at the gate around 5:40 to see the door closed :(. However, when I got to the desk I was told the flight was delayed and they hadn’t started boarding yet! (Would’ve been nice to know about that delta!). So thankfully we made our original flight, which was surprisingly empty, Becca and I each got a whole row to ourselves!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I mostly slept during the flight, we landed around 9:30am. Becca and I made our way out of the airport and picked up the car rental and then started driving to our first stop Neuschwanstein castle. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The drive took about 2 hours with some parts and the No speed limit section of the autobahn (I was able to get up to ~215 kph) which was fun. We arrived and found some parking and then hiked up to the famous bridge and viewpoint of the castle. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After walking back to the car we hit the road again. We were originally planning to see linderhof palace, but because the traffic was bad we decided to skip it and head directly to Lichtenstein. It was only about an hour and a half away so we figured we might as well see it. Plus on the way there was a via ferrata (basically a rock climbing route with iron rungs you clip into). </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The via ferrata (also know in German as klettersteig) was in a town of the boarder called Bergenz. The route was called <a href="https://www.bergsteigen.com/touren/klettersteig/kaenzele-klettersteig/">Kanzle Klettersteig</a>. We had some difficulty finding the start of the route but eventually followed some people and found it. We decided to do the easier route since it was our first one. It was so much fun! Climbing up the sheer cliff with awesome views of the city. There was a beach volleyball tournament going on in the city just across the river from us, too bad we didn’t have time otherwise I would have checked it out. </p>

In Lichtenstein we saw the capital of Vaduz and saw the church of st Florin, the castle, and the red house. </p>

<p>I was hoping to do a short little hike to a view point and some castle ruins but we were running out of daylight and decided to start the drive to Imst, a town in Austria we were staying the night in. The drive took about 1.5 hours and went through some superrrr long tunnels. I was impressed by how extensive the tunnel systems are, I guess it’s easier then driving to build roads around them. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
