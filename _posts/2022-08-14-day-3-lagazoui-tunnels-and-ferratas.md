<p>Today I worke up early, around 2am I guess the jet lag finally hit me :(. The plus side is I was able to catch up on my blog  we ended up getting up around 5:40 to load the car and eat breakfast at the hotel. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The breakfast was an assortment of pastries, eggs, yogurt, fruit, and<br />delicious hot chocolate. After breakfast we drove about 30 minutes to the falzarego pass to park for our hike. The hike we were doing was called Lagazoui (pronounced “La-Gat-zz-why”). Luckily we ended up getting a spot on the side of the road right by the trailhead! (It did involve a tight squeeze and almost driving the car off the road into the gully).</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We started up the mountain to the tunnels, on the way up we were a little confused about which way to head up as there were two different paths and we were trying to decipher a German website. Eventually we figured it out and made the right choice. About 30 minutes up the mountain through some switchbacks and we made it to the first set of tunnels.</p>

<p>The tunnels were super long, apparently they were built during WWI<br />when the Italians and the Austrians were fighting. They would mine tunnels into the mountain, hit daylight and course correct, which is why there are tons of holes poking out of the mountain in random places.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>We made our way to the summit and enjoyed the views. There was a<br>Refugio at the top where we had some food (I had a panini and apple strudle). Becca and I hiked to the summit (a short 10 minute hike from the Refugio) while Pat and Sarah rested.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After we got back we hiked down most of the way and then cut across the mountain over to the base of a via ferrata called <a href="https://www.bergsteigen.com/touren/klettersteig/via-ferrata-degli-alpini/"> Delgi Alpini</a>. The hike down was a lot of steep shale and which was a pain, lots of slipping and falling. Becca, Pat, and I did the feratta while Sarah waited behind (she was tired from all the hiking and the difficulty of this feratta was fairly high). There was a slow group in front of us to start which took them forever.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/K8iSdcZ.jpg" alt="" /></figure>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The Ferrata took foreverrr, but was really fun, there were tons of scary<br>climbs and great views. We finally made it to the summit. We enjoyed<br>the views again and took some more pictures before making our way<br>back down to reunite with Sarah.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We hiked the rest of the way back down to the car, we got water which<br>was key because we were all out from hiking all day. From there we drove to our next airbnb (about 30 minutes away) in a town called cortina di ampezo. The parking was atrocious the airbnb host definitely lied about the onsite parking. We ended up parking in front of the dumpster. The airbnb was very 'authentic everything was super outdated and it smelled a little strange. Also the ceilings were super low. The good thing was it was only a block away from the Main Street of the town.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After we got settled in we walked into town to get some groceries. Becca and I ended up making some pasta (gluten free of course) with veggies and meat and some gelato for dessert. After eating we went to bed.</p>
