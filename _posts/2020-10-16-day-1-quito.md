<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The trip started off a little rough. I got a call from the car rental company while we were waiting to board the aircraft saying that they ran out of cars so we wouldn’t be able to get one when we landed... Luckily I had made two different reservations (I forgot to cancel one). So I hastily called them to confirm that the other reservation was still good...negative, they too had given up our reservation! WTF 😫! Apparently reservation doesn’t mean the same thing in Ecuador 😂. Luckily as we were boarding I was able to get ahold of another car company that had cars, but they were in the city (not the airport) so we decided we would pick up the car the next morning. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The flights were smooth. First we flew to Charlotte, and then back down to Miami, and finally Quito! Every flight was basically full and I was stuck with the middle seat on all of them ☹️. Becca and I watched almost every episode of Utopia, really cool show, def recommend. We were a little worried on the plane because they passed out two forms for everyone to fill out and one was a declaration form stating that you promised to self isolate for 14 days :/?! Once we arrived in Quito they made us all sit and wait in the terminal while people came around to check our COVID tests and our temperature. It turned out that as long as you had a negative COVID test you didn’t have to fill out the isolation paperwork, phew!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We ended up getting out of immigration/customs around 12:30 and the hotel we were staying at sent a driver to come pick us up. We finally made it to our room around 1:30!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The next day we woke up around 8, breakfast was included and delivered outside our door. It was eggs and grilled cheese, pretty good actually! We went to the front desk to get our cab to the city and two dogs that lived on the property came to greet us so of course we gave them lots of pets!</p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_4141.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1653" width="4032" height="3024"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The ride to Quito took about 40 min by taxi. The driver took us to the Avis rental car building. Surprisingly they had a car for us! And they were super nice and helpful. The car itself was really small and as we would find out later not powerful enough for the steep mountains of Ecuador 😂. Quito is basically a massive city built in the mountains, the elevation is just over 9,000’. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Our first stop was a famous basilica, it was super cheap to enter (only $2 a person) and we got to climb the towers and these cool pathways above the vaulted ceiling of the church. </p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_4149.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1655" width="3024" height="4032"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_4155.jpg" class="size-full" width="3024" height="4032"><img src="///var/mobile/Containers/Data/Application/3BCDE303-BF4A-482F-A2AB-7FE275588BFC/Library/Caches/Media/thumbnail-p741-2208x2208.jpeg" class="size-full"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We met a super nice guy and girl working the little “restaurant” at the top of the tower, we got telecoco tea (which is the coca leaf) and cacao tea which tasted like hot chocolate. Becca tried chewing some of the bitter telecoco leaves like locals do to help with the altitude, she was not a fan (the tea was great though). </p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_0751.jpg" class="size-full" width="3088" height="2320"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Becca was not feeling well, we think because of the diamox (altitude pills). So after the church we walked down about a mile to the old town of Quito. There was supposed to be a famous Iglesia (church) there, but we couldn’t find the entrance. After a few minutes of walking around we found a nearby grocery store and got some food, water, and snacks. Becca waited with everything while I walked back and got the car. I had a minor freak out at the parking garage when the car wouldn’t open after multiple attempts. It turns out I was at the wrong car 🤦. To make matters worse the car was too weak to make it up the steep ramp out of the garage and stalled on way up the ramp. I had to let it roll back down the ramp and then speed back up it as fast as I could on my second attempt. Once I finally made it out of the garage I picked up Becca and we drove to the teleferico (cable car) to explore the nearby mountain. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The teleferico was really cool! It took us up to about 13,000' and there were great view of the city from the top and we lucked out with clear sky’s and great weather. Becca still was not feeling too well so she decided to hang out at the cafe near the cable car while I made my way further up the mountain to hike the summit of Ruccu Pinchincha. The hike up wasn’t too difficult until the very end when the clouds rolled in and it got really cold. To make matters worse right before the top, the trail I was on led to what was basically a huge sand dune which was EXHAUSTING to hike up, especially with the limited oxygen at 15,000’ I was about to turn back until I ran into some people coming down and told me that there was an easier way up on a trail just ~20’ to the right and that I was close to the summit. After a grueling ~2 hours of total hiking I finally made it to the top!</p>

<p>Great view from the top 😂</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At this point I was pretty exhausted from the hiking and altitude so I hastily made my way back down in about an hour (I descended down the sand dune path which was really fun!) and met Becca who was feeling a lot better. We took some cool pictures together and there was also a swing that had great views of the city. </p>
<p><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_0760.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1664" width="3088" height="2320"><img src="https://whereintheworldiswelter.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/img_4194.jpg" class="size-full wp-image-1660" width="3024" height="4032"></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It was around 5:30 when we finally made our way down and back to our car. According to Google we had about a 3 and a half hour drive to our next spot, Tena. It turns out...that was a lie. Getting out of Quito was a pain with the traffic and steep roads but it turned out that was the easy part! Once out of the city we had to wind through the mountains with our weak little car, and to make it even better the sun had set and the roads were filled with surprise pot holes and debris from mud slides. It was also super foggy because we were basically in the clouds of the mountain making it even more difficult to see. The trip ended up taking a solid 4 and a half hours, I’m honestly surprised our little clown car made it. We had some close calls with some pot holes and more than once I thought for sure the tire blew out. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At last we made it to our hostel in Tena around 10:30 our host was super nice and helped us order some food. I ended up getting a full size pizza and Becca had a salad- not a whole lot of gluten free options down here unfortunately. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Around midnight we went to bed. Tomorrow the plan is to go hiking and caving in Tena and then drive down to Banos. </p>
