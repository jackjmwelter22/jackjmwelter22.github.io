<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today we woke up at 7:30, got ready, and packed for our tour before heading up to breakfast at 8. We had a quick breakfast because our guide was picking us up at 8:15 to take us kayaking over the sunken ruins of Kekova.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We met our guide, Barbaros (aka Barbie) at the top of the road going to our hotel. He was in a big van and was worried it would be too steep. It was just us in the car so we ended up switching to a smaller vehicle before making the ~30 minute drive over to Kekova. Our guide was super friendly and talkative. It turns out he’s lived all over the world from California to Denver to Germany to Nepal and many others, always working as a tour guide- a really interesting life. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once we arrived at the port we stopped at the local store for some waters before meeting up with the rest of our group - a family from London area on holiday, also super friendly and nice. At the port we got all the kayaks and gear ready and then made our way into the water. Becca and I shared a double kayak. </p>

<p>Our first stop was about a 3km paddle which didn’t take too long, the water was really calm and there wasn’t too much wind, the stop was in a small Bay area of Kekova island. These ruins were not protected, so we were allowed to swim around them as well as climb and explore. </p>

<p>We put the kayaks on shore and swam around a bit. We saw some small puffer fish and a school of calamari. We also saw some of the old structures/foundations of buildings from the town. After swimming for a few minutes we went back on land to explore around the area. There were a bunch more ruins. We climbed up a small hill of ruins and could see both sides of the island it was a really cool area to explore. </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After exploring for a bit we went back to the shore and the guide gave us a small history lesson of the island. Kekova was a prominent city during the Lycian and Byzantine periods until an earthquake destroyed most of the city and submerged it underwater. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"sizeSlug":"large"} -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The guide also let us try a type of bean that is similar to caco, I thought it was really good! You just have to watch out for the hard seeds. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After talking about the history we got back in the kayaks and started paddling up the coast going over some of the ruins of the city. These ruins are protected, so we had to stay in the boat. With the water so clear you could easily see the structures. It was really cool, I took the drone out and flew to get some shots while Becca paddled for us :). Some of the structures we saw were cisterns that stored rain water, a bath house, and an old clinic where sailors had to quarantine for forty days before being allowed into the port (so they wouldn’t spread disease from town to town). </p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After the ice cream we met back up with the group down by the docks and loaded back onto the kayaks. We kayaked through a really shallow area with a tomb in the middle of it. The tombs resemble an upside down boat. They contain the body in the bottom. If you were very wealthy, your favorite slave was buried with you…apparently it was an incentive for the slave to take good care of their master so they had a long life. If a family wasn’t as well off, they would share them. So when the next family member died they would take out the bones of the person in the tomb and replace them, putting the original inhabitants bones it the catacombs. Although most of the tombs had been robbed they did find some intact. In those they found jars that were full of tears from all the family that mourned their death.</p>

<p>We continued paddling to another shallower area with a small island. We tied up the kayaks and swam for a minute. Our guide asked if anyone liked cliff jumping…yes please! We swam to the other side of the island where there was about a 15’ jump. Not the biggest I’ve done, but still fun! I jumped 4 times while Becca watched and took some photos. </p>

<p>After cliff jumping we got back in the kayaks and paddled back towards the dock we started at. Along the way we saw a massive cemetery with all those big stone monuments. In the past, they would be stand alone as a way to show wealth and importance, but once the Romans came they grouped them together into a necropolis- cities of the dead. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After making it back to the dock we put away all the kayaks and gear, and then drove back to our hotel in Kas. Overall the tour was awesome, mostly because of our guide Barbaros. If you’re ever in the area I highly recommend Lukka outdoor kayak tours. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We chilled at our hotel pool for a while and then made our way into town for dinner. We had dinner at a place a little ways up the hill called Nazende. It had a nice terrace overlooking the dock and town. The views were really nice for sunset. For dinner we had hummus as an app and I had grilled meatballs and Becca had seabrim fish. The food was decent. </p>
