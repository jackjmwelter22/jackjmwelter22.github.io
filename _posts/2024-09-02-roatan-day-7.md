<p>Day 7 Monday</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because you can't dive for 24 hours before you fly we got to sleep in today. We ran out of eggs so my mom and I walked down to the nearest mini mart to pick some up for breakfast. After we ate we left for Anthony's Key (further up the coast - on the way to Blue Island Divers) to do a dolphin swimming activity! We left the VRBO around 9:45 and drove about 15 minutes to Anthony's Key (which is a really fancy resort). My mom waited with Bennett on the mainland while the rest of us took a little boat out to the island with the dolphin enclosure. Becca had done a lot of research and apparently they are known for treating the dolphins extremely well, unlike some other resorts in the world that do dolphin activities (they were also very strict on limiting the number of people for each activity).</p>

Once by holding their dorsal fins and another time with them pushing your feet while you hold a boogie board.</p>

<p>Overall it was a super fun activity and would highly recommend trying if you have a chance! After the dolphin activity we decided to go back up to the island near Little French Key to go to AJ's Monkeys and Sloths. This was much smaller than the Gumbalimba park, but I liked it better. The cost was only $15 per person (half of Gumbalimba) and we got to see guinea pigs, sloths, macaws, and more monkeys. Holding the sloths was definitely the highlight!</p>

<p>The monkeys here were very naughty. One stole Becca's headband almost immediately once we entered there cage - thankfully the trainer was able to bribe the monkey with food to get it back :D. We spent about an hour at AJs and then drove a little further up the island to eat lunch at a place called Kristi’s Overlook. It was very tasty. I had a big beef burrito and the house margarita. There were also really nice views of the island from the deck.</p>

<p>After lunch we drove back to the VRBO and then walked up to West End to see the Roatan Chocolate Factory. They had free tours of the factory - which honestly wasn't much. They went over the process of how chocolate is made and gave you lots of samples. Honestly the samples alone were worth it. We ended up buying a few bars of chocolate to bring home after the tour.</p>
