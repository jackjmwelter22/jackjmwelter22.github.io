<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today (Friday 2/25) we got everything packed and made our way to the Savannah airport for our flight at 8pm. At the check in desk we had an “exciting” start to the trip apparently they were required to physically see the credit card I used to book the flight. We’ll just my luck I used a card that I didn’t have with me… honestly I didn’t even know which card she was talking about. I should also point out that absolutely no where in the delta emails or anywhere that I could possibly find online did it say you needed to bring the credit card to check in…apparently I was “just supposed to know”. After frantically searching the various apps I figured out it was my fidelity card and logged in to show her the statement and even a virtual version of the actual card…and after all the it STILL wasn’t good enough 😫. The only option was to get the actual card or buy a new ticket. Luckily she was able to do a one for one trade and refund the one credit card while honoring the ticket price and charging the travel credit card I had with me. QUITE the ordeal and a whole lot of stress! Worst of all once again Becca was right in wanting to get to the airport way early than I did 😊. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We got through security and to the gate with no issue so I figured we were clear to go…false. I decided to call LATAM airlines (the company operating the flight from Lima to La Paz) to try and check in, delta wasn’t able to give us our tickets for that flight since they weren’t operating it. Well, shortly after talking to the LATAM agent on the phone he says “something looks weird”. It turns out they refunded my flight and never repurchased a new one! I was able to go to the gate agent and after more stress and 45 min on the phone everything was sorted out and Becca and I were checked into the Lima-la Paz flight…phew talk about a cluster! I hung up with the LATAM agent just as our plane started to board. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The first flight was a quick uneventful hop to Atlanta airport. We landed around 9 and had to go all the way to the opposite side of the airport for our next gate. Fortunately we had plenty of time. Our second flight (Atlanta to Lima) left at midnight. My seat was right next to the bathroom door and right in front of a bulkhead…not ideal. I was sitting next to people that were going to Peru as a big group of 120 from the same company. The flight was just over 6 hours, I got very little sleep  </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We landed around 5:30am. Getting into the country was a bit of a pain, apparently you needed a declaration form, no one told us, so we had to fill out this glitchy online form that took forever, but eventually we were able to make it into Peru. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We took a ~30min cab ride from the airport to the hostel we booked in Miraflores. Even though we weren’t staying the night we wanted a place to store our bags and take a nap, well worth it! since nothing was open we relaxed until 8:30 and then ate breakfast at the hostel. I had the pariwana breakfast and pancakes. </p>

The place on google maps was wrong so we called an found out where they were flying today (~2.5 miles north). We jumped in another cabify and made it there for the flights. </p>

<p>After paratriking we took another cabify over to a main food court/shopping center area on the coast called Larcomar. We walked around and explored for a minute. The plan was originally to walk (~2 miles) to the old town of Barranco, however we were all tired so instead we took another cabify. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At Barranco we saw the bridge of sighs, which is apparently a really old bridge in Lima, not super impressive but the area was really cool. We also walked down to a mirador (lookout) to see the coast which was filled with surfers. </p>

<p>Next we walked back up slightly past the bridge to a restaurant called ayuhusca, which was recommended for it’s pisco sours (famous Peruvian drink). We all tried it except Pat who ended up trying Inca cola (another Peruvian staple) it’s a cola that tastes like bubble gum. The girls ended up wanting to eat there while pats and I decided to go to a Chinese restaurant closer by our hostel. </p>

Along the way Pat saw some art he liked (and ended up going back and buying later) and we also ran into a show about saving Mother Earth. The show was cool because it was in a mini amphitheater and tailored to kids (meaning easy to understand Spanish). </p>

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The cooking class was fun! It was just the four of us, the guide, and the chef. We ended up making was a potato dish (can’t recall the name) and lomo saltado. </p>

<p>After cooking and dinner we packed into a cabify with all our luggage (apparently cabify plus doesn’t mean anything besides an extra ~5sol charge) and drove ~40min to the airport. Getting through security was uneventful for Becca and I but apparently they were concerned Pat stole some significant artwork so they had to open up what he bought and verify that it was ok 😂. We ended up going to a random gate and resting. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Around 11:30 we woke up and made our way to the actual gate (it wasn’t assigned yet when we arrived). There they were checking everyone’s documents. We found out that you needed some special COVID insurance to board. Thankfully Becca was able to figure it out and buy some random COVID “insurance” online and we were able to board. We finally left around 1am…another rough night for sleeping!</p>
