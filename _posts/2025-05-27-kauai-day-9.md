<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Today I woke up before Becca and had some eggs for breakfast. I left around 7:10 to drive down to Koloa Landing for scuba diving, unfortunately Becca wasn’t able to scuba :). It turned out that I was the only person signed up for scuba today so it was just me and two guides: Jimi and Alejandra. Also since it was just me instead of taking a boat we were taking DPVs (diver propulsion vehicles), basically underwater scooters, to the dive sites.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We were in the water by 7:40. I played with the DPV at the surface to get the hang of it while Jimi set up the mooring buoy. We went underwater at 7:50 and then made our way out to the first dive site called rock house. Right off the landing was a massive bait ball (basically a ton of fish, like thousands), we also saw two sharks - they were a bit further away and I wasn’t able to get a picture of them quick enough.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We scootered about 10 minutes to get to the site. The scootering was so fun! I’m sold. Whenever we live somewhere where we have quick access to scuba diving I’m definitely buying one!</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The dive site was basically a bunch of big rocks in the sand. We parked the scooters at the mooring line and then swam around the site for about 30 minutes. We saw some fish, a few big turtles and some little crabs in the coral.</p>

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After exploring the site we got back in the scooters and made our way back to Koloa landing. We parked the scooters underwater at the mooring line, went back on the shore for our surface interval, and exchanged our tanks.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After about 40 minutes we got our gear back on and swam out to the mooring line to start the second dive. We descended and got the scooters and then began heading out to our next dive site called Sheraton Caverns. The dive site took about 15 minutes to get to with the scooters. Once at the site we tied up the scooters and began exploring the site.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<p>The site is actually collapsed lava tubes around 60’ under water. We swam through a bunch of the collapsed tubes. There were tons of big turtles swimming around and a lot sleeping. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>After exploring the site we got back in the scooters and made our way back to the boat ramp. We did our safety stop near the bait ball so I got to swim around all the fish. There was also a turtle swimming nearby so we said hi to him. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Once back at the dock we got all the gear out of the water and dried off. I drove back to the condo and met up with Becca. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>My back was a bit sore (I think from slipping on the mud), so we did some yoga to loosen up before heading to the pool. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the pool there were a bunch of older people doing a water aerobics class. I stayed in the hot tub and relaxed. After the pool we went back to the condo and then drove about 20 minutes to see the “historic” town of Hanapepe.</p>

<p>The beach was also right next to a small airport. Unfortunately no airplanes landed or took off while we were there. After relaxing we drove to the port to go on our whale watching tour. We arrived a little early so we got some ice cream at the store near the shop before meeting the boat captain and going down to the boat.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I really wasn’t expecting much from the tour, however it was a blast! The boat captains were pretty young guys that were hilarious and had great music selection to go along with their commentary. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There were only 8 others on the boat which meant we had a lot of space to get up and see the whales. </p>

<p>On the way out from the dock we saw a pod of dolphins, there must have been at least 2 dozen of them! It didn't take long before our captains spotted some whales and began cruising over to them. To spot the whales you basically look for their spouts which isn't as difficult as it sounds to see on the horizon.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

It was super cool to see, the whales are absolutely massive and they launch themselves out of the water and make a massive splash (all in an attempt to woo the female whales for mating of course).</p>
